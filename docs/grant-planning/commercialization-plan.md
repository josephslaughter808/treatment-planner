# Commercialization Plan

## Purpose

This document outlines the early commercialization strategy for ClearPath. It is written for grant planning and should be refined before submission.

ClearPath should be positioned as a patient-controlled interoffice and interdisciplinary medical communication platform, not as a single-specialty check-in app.

## Product Positioning

ClearPath helps providers request, receive, and review patient-approved health records across offices, disciplines, and systems.

Core value:

- Providers get needed records faster.
- Patients control what is shared.
- Offices reduce manual record chasing.
- ClearPath creates consent-scoped packages and audit trails.
- JSON acts as the internal hub language, with translators for FHIR, HL7, C-CDA, PDF, CSV, and reviewed-import workflows.

## Initial Customer Segments

### Small And Mid-Sized Provider Offices

Examples:

- Dental offices
- Oral surgery practices
- Primary care offices
- Specialty clinics
- Physical therapy and rehab offices
- Behavioral health practices where consent segmentation matters

Why they matter:

These offices often lack expensive interoperability infrastructure and still rely on phone calls, faxes, portals, PDFs, and manual patient forms.

### Multi-Discipline Referral Networks

Examples:

- Dental and oral surgery referral groups
- Primary care and specialist networks
- Community care coordination groups
- Rural care networks

Why they matter:

ClearPath becomes more valuable when records move between organizations that already refer patients to each other.

### Underserved Or Resource-Constrained Settings

Examples:

- Rural clinics
- FQHC-connected workflows
- Small practices without dedicated IT teams
- Community health programs

Why they matter:

This supports a strong grant narrative around access, coordination, and reducing administrative burden.

## Buyer And User Distinction

Buyers may include:

- Practice owners
- Office managers
- Health system innovation teams
- Care coordination leaders
- Compliance or operations leaders

Daily users may include:

- Providers
- Front desk staff
- Records staff
- Care coordinators
- Patients and caregivers

The product must satisfy both groups: buyers need ROI and compliance confidence; users need simple workflows.

## Revenue Model Options

### Option 1: Practice Subscription

Monthly subscription per practice or provider group.

Pros:

- Simple to understand
- Predictable revenue
- Fits small office buying behavior

Cons:

- Requires clear value and support
- Pricing must match practice size

### Option 2: Per-Request Or Usage-Based Pricing

Charge based on record request volume or package generation.

Pros:

- Aligns cost with usage
- Easier for low-volume practices

Cons:

- Less predictable
- Could discourage use

### Option 3: Network Or Organization Licensing

License ClearPath to a referral network, association, or care group.

Pros:

- Stronger network effects
- Easier to standardize workflows

Cons:

- Longer sales cycles
- More security and procurement requirements

### Recommended Starting Model

Start with a practice subscription plus limited usage tiers.

Example:

- Starter: small practice, limited requests
- Professional: higher request volume and more users
- Network: multi-practice or referral group

Exact pricing should wait until after Phase I discovery.

## Competitive Landscape

ClearPath should be differentiated from:

- Patient portals
- EHR-specific exchange tools
- Health information exchanges
- Fax/document request services
- Generic secure messaging tools
- Intake form tools

Differentiation:

- Patient approval is central.
- Packages are consent-scoped.
- JSON is the internal hub language.
- The workflow is interoffice and interdisciplinary.
- Small offices can participate without expensive integration projects.
- The system can support manual reviewed-import first, then deeper standards-based exchange later.

## Go-To-Market Sequence

### Phase I

- Validate problem and workflow.
- Build prototype.
- Interview providers, patients, and office staff.
- Collect letters of support or interest if possible.
- Identify strongest early customer segment.

### Phase II

- Build production-ready pilot.
- Run controlled pilots with selected sites.
- Develop pricing and sales materials.
- Prove workflow time savings and user value.
- Build compliance and security readiness.

### Post-Grant / Phase III

- Convert pilots into paying customers.
- Expand specialty and practice segments.
- Add integrations based on demand.
- Pursue strategic partnerships.
- Explore payer, health system, or network contracts.

## Commercialization Evidence To Gather

Phase I should collect:

- Provider pain points
- Current record request time and friction
- Willingness to pilot
- Willingness to pay indicators
- Most valuable record categories
- Buying decision process
- Compliance concerns
- Integration requirements

Phase II should collect:

- Time saved per request
- Request completion rates
- Patient approval rates
- Provider satisfaction
- Staff satisfaction
- Security/compliance readiness
- Pilot conversion interest

## Key Commercial Risks

- Providers may expect direct EHR integration too early.
- Patients may not understand consent choices.
- Small offices may resist new software subscriptions.
- Compliance expectations may raise cost.
- Record-holding offices may not respond without incentives.
- Network value may require multiple participating organizations.

## Risk Mitigation

- Start with human-readable packages and reviewed-import workflows.
- Keep patient approval language simple.
- Focus on high-friction record requests first.
- Build audit and consent into the core product.
- Validate pricing before Phase II scale-up.
- Choose pilot partners who already experience referral and record-request pain.

## Commercialization Thesis

ClearPath can become a lightweight patient-controlled exchange layer for practices that need safer, faster, and more auditable medical communication without waiting for every office to share the same EHR.
