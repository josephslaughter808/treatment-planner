# NIDILRR Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The National Institute on Disability, Independent Living, and Rehabilitation Research, within the Administration for Community Living, operates an annual SBIR program for commercially promising products that improve the lives of people with disabilities.

ClearPath could fit if disability communities identify cross-provider records and authorization as a consequential barrier to health, function, community living, or employment. The company should not relabel general accessibility work as disability research.

## Program Structure

NIDILRR states that it normally holds one Phase I and one Phase II competition each year.

- Phase I: feasibility projects of approximately six months, up to $100,000
- Phase II: further development for up to 24 months, up to $575,000
- Phase III: commercialization without SBIR funds

The annual Phase I announcement is normally released by early spring and closes about two months later. No open FY2026 Phase I opportunity was confirmed in this review. Applicants should search Grants.gov using the NIDILRR `BISA` opportunity identifier pattern and Assistance Listing `93.433`.

## Eligibility

NIDILRR describes eligible small businesses as:

- American-owned
- Independently operated
- For-profit
- No more than 500 employees
- Employing the principal researcher

The annual NOFO provides controlling ownership, PI, subcontracting, performance, data-rights, application, and award requirements.

## Mission Fit

Projects must address needs of people with disabilities and promote outcomes involving:

- Health and function
- Community living and participation
- Employment

Potential ClearPath problems include:

- Repeated records burden for people seeing multiple specialists
- Inaccessible portals and authorization forms
- Communication among medical, rehabilitation, behavioral, and community providers
- Patient-directed sharing of disability documentation without unnecessary disclosure
- Caregiver or supported-decision-making authority
- Transition from pediatric to adult services
- Continuity across healthcare and community supports

## Required Co-Design

A credible project should include people with relevant disabilities as paid partners in:

- Problem selection
- Requirements
- Accessibility testing
- Risk identification
- Outcome selection
- Prototype evaluation
- Commercialization and dissemination planning

Accessibility standards are a baseline, not a substitute for participatory research.

## Phase I Scope

A six-month, $100,000 project must be narrow. A feasible plan might:

1. Define one disability-centered exchange problem with community partners.
2. Build one accessible authorization and records-request prototype.
3. Test technical feasibility and critical-task accessibility.
4. Establish commercialization and Phase II milestones.

It cannot support the entire interdisciplinary platform, full HIPAA production environment, every integration, and a multi-site clinical trial.

## Team Needs

- Disability-community research partner
- Accessibility and assistive-technology expertise
- Relevant clinical or rehabilitation professional
- Human factors and usability
- Software and security engineering
- Commercialization leadership

The PI must be employed by the business and credible for the proposed research. Founder PI feasibility should be discussed with NIDILRR.

## Other NIDILRR Programs

NIDILRR also funds larger Rehabilitation Engineering Research Centers and Disability and Rehabilitation Research Projects. These commonly require substantial research infrastructure and are better approached through a university, nonprofit, or established center partnership rather than as ClearPath's first direct award.

## Contact Questions

1. When is the next Phase I SBIR expected?
2. Is accessible patient-controlled record exchange within NIDILRR's current priorities?
3. What disability outcomes would be considered responsive?
4. How much preliminary co-design is expected?
5. May the founder serve as PI with experienced research key personnel?
6. What data-rights and commercialization terms apply?

## Recommendation

Monitor the next Phase I competition and interview disability advocates, patients, caregivers, rehabilitation providers, and accessibility experts. Apply only if their input establishes a focused problem that ClearPath can address within a six-month feasibility study.
