# DoD Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Department of Defense small-business program awards contracts for technology that answers published defense needs. It is large and commercially meaningful, but it is topic-driven. ClearPath cannot submit a general proposal whenever it chooses.

The Defense Health Agency and military services could eventually need technology for care transitions, distributed care, readiness, identity, medical-data exchange, or continuity between military and civilian settings. No currently open topic clearly matching ClearPath was confirmed during this review.

## Program Structure

The current official program overview lists:

- Phase I: up to $323,090, typically 3 to 12 months
- Phase II and Direct to Phase II: up to $2,153,927, typically 15 to 24 months
- Phase II Enhancement: varies by component and may require matching non-SBIR/STTR money
- Phase III: no SBIR/STTR funds and no fixed time limit

Topic-specific instructions can set lower limits, different durations, and additional requirements. Phase II amounts may exceed the general level through waiver processes when permitted.

## Current Opportunity Process

DoD issues scheduled and annual Broad Agency Announcements and Commercial Solutions Openings. Components can release topics throughout the year. The Defense SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal, DSIP, is the authoritative source for active topics, question periods, deadlines, and submission.

FY2026 activity resumed after program reauthorization, and official notices indicate new topic releases. The live DSIP database must be searched immediately before any decision.

Relevant search terms include:

- Defense Health Agency
- health information exchange
- interoperability
- community care
- medical records
- care coordination
- patient identity
- consent or authorization
- clinical decision support
- distributed or operational medicine
- readiness

## Eligibility

For SBIR, the applicant generally must:

- Be a United States for-profit small business
- Meet qualifying United States ownership and control rules
- Have no more than 500 employees including affiliates
- Perform R&D in the United States
- Have the PI primarily employed by the small business
- Perform at least two-thirds of Phase I work and one-half of Phase II work

For STTR:

- A qualified United States research institution is required
- The small business performs at least 40%
- The research institution performs at least 30%
- The PI may be primarily employed by either organization
- An IP agreement between the parties is required

## Registrations

Plan for:

- DSIP account linked to Login.gov
- SBA Company Registry and SBC Control ID
- SAM.gov registration, UEI, and CAGE code
- Accurate firm ownership, size, and foreign-affiliation disclosures
- Any component-specific systems or representations

## Proposal And Review

Every proposal must follow the applicable BAA/CSO, component instructions, and exact topic. Volumes commonly address technical approach, statement of work, key personnel, cost, commercialization, certifications, and prior work. Page limits vary.

Review generally considers:

- Technical merit and feasibility
- Qualifications of the team
- Military relevance
- Commercialization and transition potential
- Cost realism

The government evaluates only submitted material. A good general company story cannot compensate for failure to answer the topic.

## Contract Versus Grant

DoD SBIR awards are commonly contracts. The company must execute the statement of work, meet deliverables, invoice through the required system, and communicate changes through the contracting officer.

Post-award obligations can include:

- Monthly, quarterly, and final technical reports
- Cost and schedule monitoring
- Invention reporting
- SBIR data-rights markings
- Wide Area Workflow invoicing
- Cybersecurity and controlled-information requirements
- Government accounting expectations
- Prior approval for scope, PI, subcontractor, or schedule changes

For Phase II scale, DoD recommends an adequate accounting system consistent with applicable federal requirements. DCAA may review it.

## ClearPath Military Fit

A strong topic-aligned proposal might address:

- Movement between military treatment facilities and civilian/community care
- Medical continuity during deployment, evacuation, or relocation
- Patient-authorized exchange for military families
- Trusted longitudinal information across disconnected environments
- Readiness and duty-status information exchange with strict disclosure boundaries
- Audit and provenance under contested or intermittent connectivity

These use cases create privacy, security, classification, operational, and policy requirements beyond commercial healthcare.

## CDMRP And Other DoD Health Research

Congressionally Directed Medical Research Programs fund disease- and condition-specific research through Grants.gov. FY2026 includes many named medical programs, but ClearPath is not currently a disease-specific intervention. CDMRP should not be treated as a general health-IT fund.

The Joint Warfighter Medical and Combat Readiness-Medical programs are also mission-specific. Eligibility and award types vary by announcement. ClearPath would need to satisfy the stated military medical gap, not merely provide administrative efficiency.

## Founder Salary And Costs

Reasonable labor, including founder and developer effort, may be included when necessary, allocable, allowable, and supported by timekeeping and an accepted cost proposal. Contract pricing, indirect rates, fee, travel, equipment, subcontracting, and accounting treatment follow the solicitation and negotiated contract.
