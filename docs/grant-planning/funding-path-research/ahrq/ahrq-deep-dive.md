# AHRQ Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality is part of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. It is not an NIH institute or center.

AHRQ's scientific mission fits ClearPath unusually well. Its Digital Healthcare Research program studies how digital healthcare can improve care delivery and create value for patients and families. AHRQ has specifically expressed interest in electronic health-information exchange, patient-facing digital systems, usability, human factors, implementation, and digital healthcare safety.

However, scientific fit and applicant fit are different questions.

The AHRQ research mechanisms reviewed for this report do not permit a for-profit company to lead the application. For-profit organizations may participate as consortium members or subcontractors. In addition, no currently open AHRQ funding announcement directly applicable to ClearPath was identified during this review.

Current recommendation:

- Keep NLM as the primary direct small-business target.
- Treat AHRQ as a possible future university-led or nonprofit-led research partnership.
- Contact AHRQ before investing significant application effort.
- Use AHRQ's research priorities to strengthen ClearPath's research design regardless of funder.

## What AHRQ Funds

AHRQ supports health-services and healthcare-delivery research intended to make care:

- Safer
- Higher quality
- More accessible
- More equitable
- More affordable
- More effective and efficient

AHRQ generally funds evidence generation, demonstrations, implementation research, dissemination, safety research, and evaluation. It is not primarily a startup product-development agency.

That distinction matters for ClearPath. AHRQ would be more interested in questions such as:

- Does the authorization design reduce unintended disclosure?
- Do patients understand what they are approving?
- Can staff use the system safely?
- Does the intervention reduce request delays or administrative burden?
- How should the system be implemented across different organizations?
- What sociotechnical hazards appear during use?

AHRQ would be less interested in a proposal framed only as:

- Build the application
- Add more screens
- Hire a developer
- Complete ordinary product testing
- Launch a commercial platform

## Why ClearPath Fits AHRQ Scientifically

AHRQ's Digital Healthcare Research program defines digital healthcare broadly enough to include:

- Electronic health records
- Patient portals
- Consumer health informatics applications
- Electronic exchange of health information
- Patient-provider information transfer
- Digital systems used by patients and healthcare professionals

Its digital healthcare safety notice emphasizes:

- User-centered design
- Human factors and ergonomics
- Human-computer interaction
- Usability engineering
- Sociotechnical systems
- Safe implementation
- Monitoring technology-induced errors
- Safe use by patients as well as professionals

These areas map directly to ClearPath research on:

- Section-level authorization comprehension
- Prevention of unauthorized disclosure
- Provider and staff workflow usability
- Request-status clarity
- Audit completeness
- Revocation and expiration behavior
- Provenance interpretation
- Cross-organization implementation

## PA-24-261: AI Healthcare Safety R18

### Status

Expired March 17, 2026.

The announcement originally displayed a later expiration date, but AHRQ moved expiration to March 17, 2026 through `NOT-HS-26-009`.

### Purpose

The opportunity supported research examining:

1. Whether and how breakthrough uses of AI affect patient safety.
2. How previously efficacious AI systems can be safely implemented and used.

### Why It Was Not A ClearPath Opportunity

- It did not support development of new AI systems.
- ClearPath's core innovation is not an AI safety deployment.
- It expected evaluation of actual AI deployments or previously efficacious AI systems.
- For-profit organizations could not lead.
- It is now expired.

### Historical Budget And Length

- Maximum $500,000 total costs in any one year
- Maximum $1 million total costs for the entire project
- Maximum project period of two years
- Total costs included direct and indirect costs
- No required cost sharing

These were specific to `PA-24-261`; they are not automatic limits for every future AHRQ opportunity.

### Historical Applicant Rules

Eligible lead organizations included higher-education institutions, nonprofit organizations, governments, tribal organizations, and certain other public or community entities.

For-profit organizations could participate only as consortium members or subcontractors. Applications led by for-profit organizations were not reviewed.

### Historical PI Rule

The announcement required the single PI, or multiple PIs combined, to devote at least 20% full-time effort, defined as at least eight hours per week, in each project year.

This rule was specific to the announcement and should not be assumed for future AHRQ mechanisms.

## NOT-HS-22-004: Digital Healthcare Safety

### What It Is

This is a Special Emphasis Notice, not an independent grant award.

It tells applicants that AHRQ is interested in research concerning safe digital healthcare design, implementation, usability, and use.

### Scientific Relevance

This notice is highly relevant to ClearPath because it expressly includes electronic exchange of health information and patient-facing digital systems.

It emphasizes research on:

- Technology-induced errors
- System design and usability
- Human factors
- Implementation
- User interaction
- Monitoring performance after implementation
- Safe use by patients

### Funding Problem

The notice directed applicants to three standing announcements:

- `PA-18-793`: R18 Demonstration and Dissemination Grants
- `PA-18-795`: R01 Health Services Research Projects
- `PA-18-794`: R03 Small Research Grant Program

Those standing announcements expired in 2024. The notice does not itself provide funds, a current deadline, or a submission mechanism.

### Partnership Expectation

The notice said R01 and R18 projects should include personnel from digital-health vendors and healthcare-delivery organizations. This structure would support a future partnership in which:

- A Dallas university or nonprofit healthcare research organization leads.
- ClearPath participates as the digital-health vendor and substantive research subcontractor.
- Healthcare-delivery partners participate in workflow research and implementation.

## NOT-HS-24-004: Persons Living With Disabilities

### Relevance

## Historical AHRQ Standing Mechanisms

These mechanisms are expired, but they show how AHRQ has structured investigator-initiated research.

### R03 Small Research Grant

Historical purpose:

- Discrete, well-defined health-services research projects
- Preliminary or pilot research suitable for completion within two years

Historical limits:

- Maximum $100,000 total costs for the entire project
- Maximum two-year project period
- Six-page Research Strategy after the relevant clarification
- One-page Specific Aims
- No required cost sharing

ClearPath assessment:

- Strong size for a narrow usability or workflow pilot
- Too small for the proposed $300,000 founder-and-developer budget
- For-profit company could not lead

### R01 Health Services Research Project

Historical purpose:

- Discrete, specified health-services research projects

Historical limits:

- Maximum $400,000 total costs in any year
- Maximum five-year project period
- Twelve-page Research Strategy
- One-page Specific Aims
- No required cost sharing

ClearPath assessment:

- Suitable for rigorous multi-site health-services research
- Larger and more academic than the current Phase I startup plan
- For-profit company could not lead

### R18 Demonstration And Dissemination Grant

Historical purpose:

- Demonstration, evaluation, implementation, and dissemination projects involving healthcare systems and services

Historical limits:

- Maximum $400,000 total costs in any year
- Maximum five-year project period
- Twelve-page Research Strategy
- One-page Specific Aims
- No required cost sharing

ClearPath assessment:

- Potential future fit for implementation across healthcare organizations
- Better suited after technical feasibility is established
- For-profit company could not lead

## Founder As Principal Investigator

The expired announcements allowed any individual with the necessary knowledge, skills, and resources to serve as PI, but the applicant organization had to be eligible.

Practical implications:

- The founder's lack of a traditional academic appointment is not automatically disqualifying.
- The founder would need to demonstrate research leadership, relevant expertise, and an appropriate team.
- A qualifying nonprofit lead would need to appoint or approve the PI under its policies.
- The lead organization might prefer an experienced health-services researcher as contact PI with the founder as a multiple PI, co-investigator, or subcontract lead.
- Future NOFO-specific PI effort rules must be checked.

For the reviewed AI R18, the PI team had to devote at least 20% combined full-time effort. That requirement cannot be generalized to a future NOFO.

## Salary And Technical Costs

### Founder Salary

A founder's salary may potentially be included when:

- The founder performs documented project work.
- Compensation is reasonable and consistent with the organization's practices.
- Effort is supported by the approved budget and records.
- The cost is allowable under the specific NOFO, HHS policy, and subcontract.
- The amount remains within any applicable salary cap.

The current proposed $80,000 founder salary is not automatically allowable merely because it appears in a budget. Under an AHRQ partnership, it would likely need to be part of ClearPath's subcontract budget or an eligible lead-organization appointment.

### Technical Lead

Technical-lead costs could potentially be budgeted as:

- ClearPath employee salary and fringe within a subcontract
- Lead-organization project personnel
- Consultant costs
- Contractual software-development costs

Reviewers would expect the development work to support research aims. A budget dominated by ordinary product engineering could be criticized as insufficiently research-oriented.

### Indirect Costs

Historical AHRQ caps were stated in total costs, meaning direct and indirect costs counted together against the ceiling.

The allowable rate would depend on:

- The applicant's negotiated indirect cost rate agreement
- Federal de minimis rules where applicable
- Subrecipient indirect-cost treatment
- The current NOFO and HHS Grants Policy Statement

Indirect costs cannot be added above a total-cost ceiling unless the announcement expressly permits it.

## Application And Registration Requirements

The reviewed AHRQ announcements required:

1. Active SAM.gov registration
2. Unique Entity Identifier issued through SAM.gov
3. Grants.gov registration
4. eRA Commons organization registration
5. eRA Commons account for every PI
6. Separate Signing Official and PI accounts when one person performs both roles
7. Consistent UEI across systems and application records

The announcements warned that registrations could take six weeks or more.

Applications were submitted electronically through Grants.gov and then checked and tracked in eRA Commons.

## Typical Application Components

Requirements vary by NOFO, but reviewed AHRQ applications used the SF424 Research and Related structure and included:

- SF424 cover information
- Project Summary/Abstract
- Project Narrative
- Facilities and Other Resources
- Equipment
- Senior/Key Person profiles
- Biosketches
- Detailed R&R budget
- Subaward budgets
- Budget justification
- Specific Aims
- Research Strategy
- Bibliography and References Cited
- Letters of Support from participating entities
- Resource Sharing or Data Management Plan
- Human Subjects and Clinical Trials information where applicable
- Authentication of key resources where applicable
- Assignment Request form

Historical page limits included:

- Specific Aims: one page
- R01 and R18 Research Strategy: twelve pages
- R03 Research Strategy: six pages after clarification

Every future NOFO must be checked because AHRQ warns that its page limits may differ from NIH limits.

## Human Subjects And IRB

Interviews and usability studies may constitute human-subjects research.

Reviewed AHRQ requirements included:

- PHS Human Subjects and Clinical Trials study records
- Protection of Human Subjects information
- Inclusion of relevant priority populations
- IRB review before research begins where required
- Single-IRB requirements for applicable cooperative research
- Confidentiality safeguards for identifiable people and organizations

The reviewed announcement did not require IRB approval before peer review, but encouraged early initiation to avoid project delays.

## Priority Populations

AHRQ expects applicants to consider priority populations, including:

- Inner-city populations
- Rural populations
- Low-income populations
- Minority populations
- Women
- Children
- Older adults
- People with special healthcare needs
- People with disabilities
- People requiring chronic or end-of-life care
- Other underserved communities identified in applicable policy

For ClearPath, Dallas could support meaningful research involving safety-net care, complex care, disability, low health literacy, or fragmented transitions. These populations must be included because they are relevant to the research, not used as decorative grant language.

## Data Management And Confidentiality

AHRQ requires a Data Management Plan for new and competing grants and research contracts, or a justified statement explaining why data management is not possible.

The plan should address:

- Data collected
- Storage and protection
- Documentation
- Sharing schedule
- Dataset format
- Access restrictions
- Data-sharing agreements
- Privacy and confidentiality
- Retention and destruction

AHRQ has a specific confidentiality statute. Identifiable information about individuals or establishments obtained through AHRQ-supported activities may be used only for the purpose for which it was supplied and generally may not be released without consent.

Applications must explain:

- Who can access identifiable data
- How identifiers are separated or restricted
- How machine-readable files are protected
- How subcontractors and vendors follow equivalent requirements
- How HIPAA and security requirements apply

For ClearPath Phase I, synthetic medical records would reduce PHI risk, but participant identity, interview data, recordings, and employer information could still be sensitive research data.

## Health IT Requirements

The reviewed AHRQ award terms required funded health IT activities to consider applicable standards and implementation specifications adopted in federal health IT regulations.

ClearPath should expect reviewers or program staff to ask about:

- FHIR
- US Core
- USCDI
- C-CDA
- TEFCA
- Patient access
- Provenance
- Information blocking
- Accessibility
- Interoperability testing
- Security and privacy

The ClearPath canonical JSON package should be presented as an internal model that maps to standards, not a replacement for federal interoperability standards.

## Intellectual Property And Public Access

Under the reviewed AHRQ terms:

- Recipients could copyright or seek patents for funded products and materials, including software and documentation.
- AHRQ retained a royalty-free, nonexclusive, irrevocable license to reproduce, publish, use, or disseminate funded materials for purposes consistent with its statutory responsibilities.
- AHRQ could make products, databases, results, and algorithms available for verification or replication, subject to applicable protections.
- Peer-reviewed manuscripts arising from AHRQ support were subject to public-access requirements through PubMed Central.

This does not mean AHRQ automatically owns ClearPath. It does mean the precise award terms, background intellectual property, newly developed intellectual property, software licensing, data rights, and subcontract terms require attorney review before accepting an award.

## Reporting And Award Administration

The reviewed AHRQ grant required:

- Annual progress reports
- Annual Federal Financial Reports
- Performance reporting tied to project aims
- Subaward reporting where thresholds apply
- Final progress report
- Final Federal Financial Report
- Final invention statement
- Closeout reports within the specified period
- Compliance with the HHS Grants Policy Statement and Notice of Award

For `PA-24-261`, annual progress reports were due three months before the next budget period. Final reports were due within 120 days after the project period ended.

Future announcements may change these details.

## Review Criteria

Reviewed AHRQ applications were scored on:

- Overall impact
- Significance
- Investigator qualifications
- Innovation
- Approach
- Environment

AHRQ-specific reviewer concerns are likely to include:

- Importance to healthcare delivery
- Scientific rigor
- Safe implementation
- Human factors
- Meaningful healthcare-organization involvement
- Patient involvement
- Generalizable evidence
- Feasible dissemination
- Inclusion of priority populations
- Data management and confidentiality

For ClearPath, the team gap is significant. AHRQ reviewers would likely expect an experienced health-services researcher, human-factors specialist, clinical partner, and analysis lead in addition to the founder and developer.

## Realistic AHRQ Timeline

When an appropriate NOFO is open, a realistic preparation sequence would be:

### Months 1-2

- Confirm opportunity and fit with AHRQ staff
- Recruit eligible lead institution
- Select experienced PI or multiple-PI structure
- Define ClearPath subcontract role
- Obtain partner commitments

### Months 2-4

- Finalize aims, methods, analysis, and human-subjects strategy
- Negotiate budget and indirect costs
- Prepare data-management and security plans
- Draft application components

### Months 4-5

- Complete institutional review and submission
- Correct Grants.gov or eRA validation errors before deadline

### After Submission

- Peer review timing depends on the NOFO
- Advisory or agency review follows where applicable
- Earliest start date may be several months after review
- Contract and subcontract negotiations may continue before project launch

The expired AI R18 estimated scientific review about four months after receipt and earliest start about four months after peer review. This is an example, not a universal AHRQ schedule.

## AHRQ Contract And Procurement Path

AHRQ also uses contracts and procurements. A for-profit company can potentially compete when a solicitation permits it.

This path differs from a research grant:

- The government defines the work it wants purchased.
- Requirements and deliverables may be more prescriptive.
- Opportunities are posted through SAM.gov or specified procurement channels.
- The company must respond to an active solicitation rather than submit an investigator-initiated research idea.

No matching active AHRQ procurement was verified in this review. SAM.gov monitoring should be added to the funding workflow.

## Final Recommendation

AHRQ should remain on the ClearPath funding map, but not as the current lead application.

The near-term strategy should be:

1. Apply directly through an NIH small-business mechanism, with NLM as the primary target.
2. Incorporate AHRQ digital safety and human-factors research principles.
3. Recruit a Dallas health-services or informatics research partner.
4. Contact AHRQ to ask about upcoming mechanisms.
5. Reconsider AHRQ when a compatible opportunity is officially open.

This conclusion should change if AHRQ confirms a new small-business-compatible mechanism or an eligible Dallas partner agrees to lead a well-matched research application.
