# PCORI Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute funds comparative clinical effectiveness research that helps patients, caregivers, clinicians, and health systems choose among available care strategies. For-profit research organizations may apply.

PCORI is not a startup product-development fund. ClearPath becomes relevant when the platform is mature enough to compare two established record-access or care-delivery strategies across real clinical settings with meaningful patient partnership.

## Upcoming Cycle 3 2026 Route

The Broad Pragmatic Studies PFA is scheduled to:

- Open August 4, 2026
- Hold an applicant town hall August 13, 2026
- Require Letters of Intent by September 9, 2026 at 5 p.m. Eastern
- Notify LOI decisions October 14, 2026
- Require invited applications by January 12, 2027
- Announce awards around August 2027
- Begin awards no earlier than approximately December 2027

PCORI plans up to $120 million in direct-cost commitments across three categories:

- Category 1: up to $5 million direct costs
- Category 2: over $5 million and up to $12 million direct costs
- Category 3: up to $12 million for qualifying national PCORnet studies
- Maximum project period: five years

These amounts reflect large clinical research programs, not an amount ClearPath should presently request.

## Eligibility

PCORI generally permits applications from:

- For-profit and nonprofit research organizations
- Universities and colleges
- Hospitals, laboratories, and healthcare systems
- Public-sector research organizations
- Government entities

Individuals cannot apply. The applicant must be able to comply with PCORI methodology, transparency, data-sharing, privacy, ethics, public-availability, and contract terms.

PCORI does not require an academic lead, but ClearPath currently lacks the comparative-effectiveness research capacity to lead responsibly without experienced clinical-research partners.

## What PCORI Requires Scientifically

A responsive study compares at least two available health treatments, services, or care-delivery strategies. It must evaluate benefits and harms that matter to patients.

PCORI will not fund a study whose main purpose is:

- Building the first version of ClearPath
- Proving that the app technically works
- Conducting ordinary customer discovery
- Testing a single intervention without a meaningful comparator
- Performing implementation alone without comparative clinical effectiveness
- Developing research methods without the required patient-centered question

## Future ClearPath Study Models

After a successful pilot, possible comparisons include:

- ClearPath patient-controlled requests versus current release-of-information workflow
- Granular authorization versus broad document-level authorization
- Patient-mediated exchange versus staff-only retrieval
- Structured provenance package versus unstructured document transfer
- Different established care-transition models using ClearPath as one component

Outcomes could include clinically meaningful delays, repeated testing, medication reconciliation, care-plan concordance, patient trust, caregiver burden, adverse events, and access. Administrative time alone is unlikely to carry a PCORI application.

## Patient Partnership

PCORI expects patients and other stakeholders to be meaningful research partners, not only participants. They should influence:

- Research questions
- Comparator selection
- Outcomes
- Recruitment and retention
- Interpretation
- Dissemination
- Governance and decision-making

ClearPath should begin a compensated patient advisory process before seeking PCORI funds.

## Data And Institutional Requirements

A competitive study would likely need:

- Multiple clinical sites
- Established interventions and workflows
- Adequate sample size and statistical power
- IRB and human-subject protections
- Data coordinating capacity
- Clinical and patient-reported outcomes
- Privacy and ethical controls
- Milestones and recruitment feasibility
- A robust contracting and subaward structure

These are Phase II or later capabilities.

## Current Cycle Fit

Cycle 3 includes broad care-delivery comparisons, but its highlighted areas emphasize specific clinical populations. ClearPath should not force itself into diabetes, cancer survivorship, pain, pregnancy, intellectual disability, or another theme without a real clinical comparison and partners.

The PFA also permits applicants to identify other research themes. That flexibility does not lower the comparative-effectiveness standard.

## Contact Questions

1. Could patient-controlled record exchange be treated as a healthcare-delivery system intervention?
2. What clinical outcomes would make such a comparison patient-centered and responsive?
3. How mature must both comparator strategies be before application?
4. Can a small technology company serve as prime with health systems as subcontractors?
5. What patient-partner governance is expected before the LOI?
6. Would an implementation PFA be more appropriate after relevant PCORI evidence exists?

## Recommendation

Place PCORI on the Phase II evidence roadmap, not the immediate funding calendar. Use the next year to establish pilot partners, patient governance, comparators, clinical outcomes, and research leadership.
