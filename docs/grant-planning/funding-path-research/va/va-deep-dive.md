# VA Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Department of Veterans Affairs has a powerful ClearPath use case: Veterans often receive care across VA and community providers, creating continuity, authorization, and information-exchange challenges. However, VA's major research programs are primarily intramural and are not a direct startup Phase I grant program.

VA should be approached as a research partner, pilot setting, innovation collaborator, and future customer. Initial company R&D should be financed elsewhere.

## VA Research Funding

VA Health Systems Research, QUERI, Biomedical Laboratory R&D, Clinical Science R&D, and related programs generally fund eligible VA investigators.

QUERI expressly states that it is intramural and all PIs must be VA employees. Broader VA research eligibility commonly requires a substantial VA appointment, appropriate credentials, VA research space, local research-office support, and submission through VA processes. Some details are available only on the VA intranet.

The founder cannot lead a normal VA intramural research award solely as the CEO of ClearPath. An eligible VA investigator could lead a project in which ClearPath has an approved external role.

## Current BRAVE Program

VA's FY2027 BRAVE Technology Transfer program illustrates the intramural model:

- Up to $100,000
- Supports commercialization of VA-owned or jointly owned intellectual property
- Eligible inventors are VA employees who disclosed an invention to VA
- Current full application deadline: July 10, 2026, after invitation
- Award period runs to September 30, 2027 under the published schedule

ClearPath is not eligible unless the technology is VA-owned or jointly owned and a qualifying VA inventor leads. Entering such an arrangement would also require careful IP review.

## Practical External-Company Routes

### VA Investigator Partnership

An eligible VA investigator leads research or implementation evaluation. ClearPath may serve as a collaborator, subcontractor, vendor, or technology provider subject to VA approvals.

### Procurement

VA may purchase technology through federal acquisition. ClearPath would need:

- Active SAM.gov registration and UEI
- Appropriate NAICS and representations
- Market research and contracting engagement
- Cybersecurity and privacy readiness
- Accessibility compliance
- Relevant past performance or a strong teaming partner
- Potential VA-specific verification and authority-to-operate work

### Cooperative Research Or Technology Agreement

VA research and technology-transfer offices may support external collaboration under appropriate agreements. Terms concerning data, IP, publications, security, and commercialization must be negotiated.

### Innovation Ecosystem And Challenges

VHA innovation programs can connect companies to operational problems and pilots. These are not guaranteed grants, and availability changes.

## Dallas Strategy

The founder's Dallas location can help relationship development but does not create eligibility. A practical sequence is:

1. Identify relevant VA North Texas clinicians, health-services researchers, informatics leaders, community-care staff, and innovation personnel.
2. Conduct discovery on record requests, community-care transitions, patient authorization, and missing-information harm.
3. Define a small research or pilot question that protects Veteran data and minimizes operational burden.
4. Let the VA partner determine the correct research, innovation, privacy, security, and procurement pathway.

Do not approach clinical staff with an assumption that they can independently approve software access to VA systems or data.

## ClearPath Research Questions For VA

- Can patient-mediated requests reduce missing community-care records?
- Does granular authorization improve Veteran understanding and trust?
- Can provenance and status tracking reduce duplicate work?
- What safety risks arise when VA and community records conflict?
- What exchange functions are already addressed by VA, TEFCA, HIEs, or existing vendors?
- What workflow and identity constraints are unique to Veterans and caregivers?

## Data, Security, And Regulatory Burden

A VA deployment can involve:

- Federal information-security requirements
- VA privacy and security reviews
- Federal records and data-location controls
- Section 508 accessibility
- Human-subject and research approvals
- Data-use agreements
- Contract clauses and incident reporting
- Integration and authority-to-operate processes

These requirements can exceed ordinary HIPAA readiness and should be included in timeline and budget planning.

## IP And Commercialization Risk

Before accepting VA-funded development or co-invention arrangements, counsel should review:

- Background IP
- Subject inventions
- Government licenses and data rights
- Publication rights
- Commercialization obligations
- Ownership of integrations and improvements

## Contact Questions

1. Is the problem best treated as research, quality improvement, innovation, or procurement?
2. Must a VA employee serve as PI or project owner?
3. What external-company role is allowed?
4. What approvals are needed for Veteran data and system integration?
5. What existing VA tools overlap with ClearPath?
6. What contracting vehicle or teaming path is realistic for an early-stage company?

## Recommendation

Begin customer and partner discovery with VA North Texas, but keep VA outside the immediate $300,000 grant plan. A VA letter of support or future pilot could materially strengthen NLM or NSF applications.
