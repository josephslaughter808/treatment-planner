# NIA Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The National Institute on Aging supports small-business digital health, care-delivery, care-coordination, aging-in-place, and caregiver technologies. ClearPath could be a strong fit if its research genuinely addresses older adults' fragmented care and authorization needs.

NIA should not redefine the entire company as an aging product. A Phase I project can study one high-value population while preserving a broader interdisciplinary platform.

## Current Route

NIA participates in NIH small-business funding. The active Parent SBIR `PA-27-100` is the likely route, subject to the current participation table. Parent STTR `PA-27-102` may support a qualified research-institution partnership.

General NIH rules for eligible company ownership, size, PI employment, workshare, domestic performance, registrations, and application content apply.

## Relevant NIA Interests

NIA's current interests include:

- Digital health products for older adults
- Technologies that improve care delivery and management
- Care coordination
- Aging in place of choice
- Reduction of caregiving burden
- Accessible and cost-effective commercial solutions
- Data-science technologies for aging and health outcomes
- Interventions that improve independence and quality of life

## Potential ClearPath Study

A strong Phase I concept might evaluate patient- and caregiver-mediated record exchange during transitions among:

- Primary care
- Specialists
- Hospitals
- Rehabilitation or skilled nursing
- Home health
- Community-based services

Research questions could include:

- Can older adults and authorized caregivers correctly understand and control granular disclosure?
- Does the workflow reduce missing information or repeated records work?
- How should proxy, guardian, caregiver, and dependent authority be represented?
- What accessibility features are required for vision, cognition, dexterity, and digital-literacy differences?
- How should conflicting or stale records be communicated safely?
- Can the intervention reduce caregiver administrative burden?

## Budget And Project Period

NIA's current small-business considerations state:

- Non-AD/ADRD projects: up to $500,000 total costs in Phase I and $2.5 million in Phase II
- AD/ADRD projects: up to $700,000 in Phase I and $3 million in Phase II
- Phase I periods: up to two years
- Phase II periods: up to three years

These are institute limits, not automatic award sizes. The requested amount must be necessary, reasonable, and supported by the research plan.

ClearPath should not use an Alzheimer's label unless the intervention and research genuinely concern AD/ADRD. The broader older-adult care-delivery route can support a substantial non-AD/ADRD Phase I.

NIA currently accepts Phase IIB and Commercialization Readiness Pilot applications and accepts clinical trials through its small-business mechanisms, subject to the opportunity.

## Team And Partner Needs

The team should include:

- Geriatrics or aging-health expertise
- Caregiver and patient partners
- Health-services or implementation research
- Accessibility and human factors
- Informatics and interoperability
- Clinical partners across at least two care settings
- Statistical and qualitative-methods support

Dallas offers potential access to academic, health-system, rehabilitation, home-health, aging-services, and VA-adjacent communities, but no organization should be named as a partner without written agreement.

## Accessibility And Ethics

The design must account for:

- Supported decision-making and proxy authority
- Cognitive impairment without assuming incapacity
- Caregiver access boundaries
- Vision, hearing, dexterity, literacy, and language
- Mobile and non-mobile workflows
- Fraud, coercion, and unauthorized family access
- Emergency and post-discharge timing

These are central research issues, not a late compliance checklist.

## Commercialization Fit

Potential buyers and partners include health systems, clinically integrated networks, Medicare-focused providers, home-health organizations, post-acute providers, accountable-care organizations, and aging-service networks.

Customer discovery must test who pays for exchange, whether existing HIE/EHR tools already solve the problem, and whether reduced delay or burden creates measurable economic value.

3. What preliminary data and aging-partner evidence are expected?

## Recommendation

Develop a one-page NIA concept after interviewing older adults, caregivers, and geriatric/post-acute professionals. NIA can be a top-four route if those interviews confirm a distinct, consequential aging use case.
