# ARPA-H Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

ARPA-H funds bold, outcome-driven programs that aim to transform health, not conventional incremental grants. It commonly uses Other Transactions, cooperative agreements, and contracts with negotiated milestones and substantial program involvement.

ClearPath's national interoperability goal is directionally relevant, especially to the Resilient Systems and Scalable Solutions mission offices. The company is not currently ready to make ARPA-H its primary funding path, and the active small-business topics reviewed do not match the product.

## Current Small-Business Solicitation

ARPA-H's current 2026 SBIR/STTR contract process lists:

- Phase I generally up to $600,000
- Phase II generally up to $3.5 million
- Solution Summary deadline: July 10, 2026
- Pitch and cost-proposal deadline: September 9, 2026, for invited offerors

The seven current topics concern fertility testing, bioadhesives, toxin removal, endometriosis, cell lineage, autoimmune diagnostics, and virtual-brain/neurosurgical robotics. None is a credible ClearPath match.

Topic solicitations must be followed exactly. ARPA-H will not fund an unrelated interoperability product under a superficially similar health label.

## Broader Mission Office Openings

ARPA-H's Mission Office Innovative Solutions Openings accept Solution Summaries on a rolling basis under their current terms. The two most relevant offices are:

- Resilient Systems: stronger, more integrated, and more reliable health systems
- Scalable Solutions: approaches that can reach people and communities at national scale, including data and information challenges

A Solution Summary receives written feedback before a full proposal. Positive feedback is not an award commitment.

## ARPA-H Fit Test

An ARPA-H concept should answer questions such as:

- What is the current assumption that must be overturned?
- Why can the result not be achieved through normal product development or existing federal programs?
- What measurable national health outcome changes if the program succeeds?
- What are the aggressive go/no-go milestones?
- Why is the team uniquely able to execute?
- How will the solution scale and transition after ARPA-H funding?

For ClearPath, an appropriately ambitious thesis might concern a national patient-controlled trust and authorization layer that functions across fragmented care settings without requiring replacement of existing EHRs. That claim would need serious technical, policy, clinical, and deployment evidence.

## Eligibility And Workshare

For ARPA-H SBIR:

- The applicant is a qualifying United States small business with no more than 500 employees.
- Qualifying United States ownership and control rules apply.
- Research is generally performed in the United States.
- The PI's primary employment is with the small business.
- The small business normally performs more than two-thirds of Phase I work and at least half of Phase II work.

For STTR:

- A qualified research-institution partner is required.
- The small business performs at least 40%.
- The research institution performs at least 30%.
- The PI may be primarily employed by either party, subject to the solicitation.

## Award Terms

ARPA-H terms are opportunity-specific. Applicants should expect:

- Negotiated technical milestones and deliverables
- Go/no-go decisions tied to measurable performance
- Cost and schedule reporting
- Intellectual-property and data-rights provisions
- Security, privacy, human-subjects, and regulatory requirements where applicable
- Active engagement with an ARPA-H program manager
- Transition and commercialization obligations

This is not dependable general operating support. Failure to meet milestones can end or redirect a program.

## Team And Readiness Gaps

Before an ARPA-H submission, ClearPath would need:

- A nationally consequential technical thesis
- Preliminary evidence
- Healthcare-system and data partners
- Senior informatics, security, human-factors, clinical, and commercialization leadership
- Quantitative milestones
- A deployment and regulatory pathway
- Capacity to manage a complex federal contract or agreement
