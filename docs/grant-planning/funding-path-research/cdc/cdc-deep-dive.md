# CDC Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention operates an SBIR program for commercializable innovation that meets federal public-health R&D needs. CDC participates in the current NIH/CDC Parent SBIR opportunity `PA-27-100`.

ClearPath's general healthcare interoperability mission is not by itself a CDC mission fit. A CDC proposal must advance population health, prevention, surveillance, outbreak response, occupational safety, laboratory reporting, or another stated CDC priority.

## Current SBIR Route

### Parent SBIR `PA-27-100`

- Status: active through April 6, 2027
- Mechanisms: Phase I `R43` and Phase II `R44`; exact Fast-Track acceptance depends on CDC and the announcement
- Phase I: CDC describes awards up to $323,090, typically six months but potentially up to two years
- Phase II: CDC describes awards up to $2,153,927 for up to two years
- CDC may use an SBA waiver above standard limits for identified CDC topic areas
- Phase III commercialization is not funded with SBIR money

The live announcement and CDC's current topic list control. Dollar figures are ceilings, not expected awards.

## Eligibility

Only qualifying United States small businesses may lead CDC SBIR applications. NIH/HHS small-business rules apply, including ownership, size, domestic performance, PI employment, registration, and workshare requirements.

CDC does not operate an STTR program in the same manner as participating NIH institutes. The current agency participation table must be checked rather than assuming STTR, Direct Phase II, Fast-Track, Phase IIB, or commercialization supplements are available.

## Potential ClearPath Public-Health Uses

A credible CDC-aligned project could concern:

- Timely, standards-based exchange of reportable conditions
- Patient-mediated retrieval of immunization or screening information
- Continuity during outbreaks, disasters, displacement, or cross-jurisdiction care
- Occupational-health record coordination when aligned with NIOSH priorities
- Public-health laboratory and clinical-data exchange
- Prevention-program referrals and closed-loop coordination
- Data quality, provenance, and completeness for public-health action

The project must still preserve ClearPath's core product direction. It should not bolt on a nominal dashboard simply to claim CDC relevance.

## Current Fit Assessment

### Strengths

- CDC has an active direct small-business mechanism.
- Interoperability and provenance can matter to public-health data quality.
- Commercialization and public benefit are explicit SBIR goals.

### Weaknesses

- ClearPath is currently designed around interoffice clinical record requests, not public-health operations.
- A strong proposal may require a health department, laboratory, or public-health network partner.
- CDC topic alignment can be narrower than NLM's informatics mission.
- NIOSH topics focus occupational safety and health and are not automatically relevant.

## Application And Review

The application uses the NIH/HHS SBIR submission ecosystem and current Forms I guide. Required registrations include SAM.gov/UEI, Grants.gov, eRA Commons, and the SBA Company Registry.

Review considers scientific and technical merit, significance to CDC mission, innovation, approach, team, environment, human-subject protections, and commercialization potential. Programmatic relevance can determine funding even after peer review.

## Grants And Cooperative Agreements Outside SBIR

CDC also funds research and non-research grants and cooperative agreements. Some allow for-profit applicants, while many are restricted to public-health departments, governments, universities, nonprofits, or specifically qualified organizations.

Each NOFO controls eligibility, funding, period, application content, and review. CDC describes a typical lifecycle of several months before award and one to five years post-award, but that is not a promise for any ClearPath opportunity.

## Founder Salary, Indirect Costs, And Compliance

For SBIR, reasonable founder and technical-team compensation may be allowable when tied to documented research effort. NIH/HHS indirect-cost rules and the parent announcement apply.

CDC awards require accurate timekeeping, financial controls, progress reporting, human-subject oversight where applicable, privacy and security controls, and compliance with the Notice of Award. Public-health data may create additional agreements and jurisdictional rules beyond HIPAA.

## Contact Questions

Send a concise concept to `SBIR@cdc.gov` and ask:

1. Which current CDC topic, center, institute, or office best matches the use case?
2. Is patient-controlled cross-provider exchange sufficiently connected to a CDC research need?
3. Would a health-department or public-health laboratory partner be expected?
4. Does CDC accept Fast-Track under `PA-27-100`?
5. What budget and project period are appropriate for the selected topic?
6. Is NLM the more appropriate primary assignment?

## Recommendation

Do not submit a generic ClearPath proposal to CDC. Preserve CDC as a conditional option if program staff identifies a specific public-health topic and a suitable implementation partner.
