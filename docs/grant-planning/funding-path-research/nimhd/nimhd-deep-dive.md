# NIMHD Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

The National Institute on Minority Health and Health Disparities is a strong potential NIH home for ClearPath because its current small-business priorities explicitly include digital health, electronic health records, biomedical informatics platforms, patient-clinician communication, telehealth, healthcare access, and technologies supporting underserved providers.

The fit is not automatic. NIMHD funds research intended to reduce documented health disparities among NIH-designated populations. ClearPath must identify a population, disparity mechanism, community context, and measurable health-access or care-quality outcome. A generic claim that fragmented records affect everyone will not be enough.

## Current Route

The practical route is the active NIH Parent SBIR `PA-27-100`, subject to NIMHD participation and institute confirmation. General NIH small-business requirements apply:

- Eligible United States for-profit small business
- No more than 500 employees including affiliates
- Qualifying United States ownership and control
- Domestic performance except for approved exceptions
- SBIR PI primarily employed by the company during the award
- Small-business workshare requirements
- SAM.gov, UEI, Grants.gov, eRA Commons, and SBA registrations

STTR through `PA-27-102` may be preferable if a university or research institution will perform a major share of community-engaged research.

## Relevant NIMHD Interests

Current NIMHD small-business interests include products or services that:

- Improve access to and use of healthcare technologies
- Strengthen patient-clinician communication
- Improve chronic-disease knowledge and management
- Support telehealth, telemedicine, remote monitoring, and mobile health
- Use EHR systems, biomedical informatics, and data platforms to improve dissemination and use of health information
- Support federal safety-net and rural programs, including HRSA, CMS, ACL, FQHCs, critical-access hospitals, and rural providers
- Improve healthcare access and research participation for women experiencing disparities

## ClearPath Research Models

Possible responsive projects include:

### Safety-Net Care Transitions

Test whether patient-controlled record requests improve continuity between an FQHC, specialty practice, hospital, and community provider serving a defined disparity population.

### Limited Digital Access

Design and evaluate authorization workflows for people with limited broadband, device access, health literacy, English proficiency, or portal access.

### Complex Cross-System Care

Study whether granular authorization and provenance reduce missing records, repeated tests, delays, or administrative burden for a population with documented fragmentation.

### Women's Health Access

Evaluate interdisciplinary record exchange for a defined women's-health transition where current fragmentation contributes to unequal access or outcomes.

These should remain examples until stakeholder discovery establishes the actual problem and community priorities.

## Required Research Features

A credible NIMHD application should include:

- A recognized population experiencing a documented disparity
- Evidence that record fragmentation contributes to the disparity
- Community and clinical partners involved before submission
- Accessible and culturally appropriate design
- Measures of access, timeliness, safety, understanding, burden, and equity
- Analysis of whether benefits and errors differ across subgroups
- A plan to avoid worsening digital exclusion
- A commercialization model that can serve resource-constrained organizations

## Budget And Duration

The NIH parent announcement's standard guideline amounts apply unless a justified institute-approved waiver is available. NIMHD identifies approved topic areas for awards over statutory budget guidelines, including several that may encompass ClearPath, but approval is not automatic.

The proposal should use a budget driven by aims and milestones. Program staff must confirm whether NIMHD would consider a request above the standard Phase I guideline and what evidence is required.

NIMHD accepts Phase IIB SBIR and the Commercialization Readiness Pilot under its current institute considerations. It also accepts clinical trials through SBIR and STTR mechanisms, subject to the selected opportunity.

## Founder And Team

The founder may serve as PI if NIH SBIR employment and capability rules are met. The team should add:

- Health-disparities research expertise
- Community-engaged research leadership
- Clinical workflow expertise
- Human factors and accessibility
- Biomedical informatics
- Quantitative and qualitative methods
- A community advisory structure with compensated participation where allowable

Token representation or late-stage recruitment of a named population would weaken the proposal and the product.

## Major Risks

- Choosing a population because it seems fundable rather than because the team has a trusted relationship
- Measuring usability without measuring disparity-relevant outcomes
- Assuming a smartphone-first workflow is equitable
- Treating race or geography as a sufficient explanation of the disparity
- Building a platform whose eventual price excludes the intended safety-net customers
- Failing to distinguish health-services research from routine product development

## Contact Questions

1. Does patient-controlled cross-organization record exchange fit NIMHD's informatics priorities?
2. Which disparity populations and settings are currently highest priority?
3. Would SBIR or STTR be more appropriate for a community/university partnership?
4. Is a budget above the standard Phase I guideline potentially justified for this topic?
5. What community-engagement evidence is expected at submission?
6. Which study section and expertise should be anticipated?

## Recommendation

Move NIMHD into the top tier, but do not select it until two or more community and safety-net partners validate a specific disparity-centered problem. NLM remains the cleaner mission fit for a specialty-neutral platform; NIMHD may become equally strong when the equity study is real and partner-led.
