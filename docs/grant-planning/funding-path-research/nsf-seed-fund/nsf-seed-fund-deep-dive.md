# NSF Seed Fund Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

America's Seed Fund powered by NSF is a strong alternative to NIH because it supports high-risk technology R&D across markets and places heavy weight on commercial potential. It can fund ClearPath without requiring the project to fit one NIH institute's biomedical research mission.

The tradeoff is that NSF will not fund routine application development. ClearPath must identify a real technical innovation and uncertainty that cannot be resolved through ordinary engineering.

## Active Solicitation

### `NSF 26-510`

- Status: active
- Phase I: up to $305,000 total
- Phase I period: 6 to 18 months
- Phase II: up to $1.25 million, typically 24 months
- Fast-Track: up to $1,555,555 across phases
- Current full-proposal dates: July 27, 2026; November 4, 2026; March 4, 2027, with later annual cycles listed in the solicitation
- Cost sharing: voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited

The award total includes direct costs, indirect costs, fee, and optional program allowances described in the solicitation. The budget must therefore reserve enough money for research labor while accurately including overhead and other required items.

## Mandatory Project Pitch

A company cannot simply submit a Phase I proposal. It first submits a Project Pitch covering:

- The technological innovation
- Technical objectives and challenges
- Market opportunity
- Company and team

NSF generally responds in approximately one to two months. An invitation allows a full proposal but does not promise funding. A company may submit no more than two pitches in a program year and no more than three pitches for substantially the same technology.

## Eligibility

The applicant must be an eligible United States small business with no more than 500 employees. The PI must have a legal right to work in the United States and be at least 51% employed by the small business during the award. No specific degree is required.

Minimum PI effort is stated in the current solicitation:

- Phase I and Phase II: at least one calendar month for each six months of project duration
- Fast-Track: at least three calendar months for each six months

The exact ownership, affiliate, domestic-performance, and subcontracting rules in the solicitation control.

## Registrations And Submission

Required systems include:

- SAM.gov and UEI
- Research.gov organization and user registrations
- SBA Company Registry and SBC Control ID
- Login.gov credentials as required by connected systems

The Project Pitch should be prepared while registrations are processing. Full proposals are submitted in Research.gov.

## ClearPath's Technical Case

A credible NSF pitch might focus on one of these hard problems:

- Computable patient authorization that remains precise across heterogeneous clinical data and organizations
- Automated semantic reconciliation with measurable preservation of meaning and provenance
- Distributed trust and audit methods for exchange among organizations without a common record system
- Privacy-preserving disclosure minimization that still supplies clinically sufficient context
- Robust failure recovery and verifiable delivery for asynchronous healthcare record exchange

The pitch should not claim that using JSON itself is the innovation. JSON is a serialization format. The innovation must concern semantics, trust, policy, privacy, verification, or another unresolved technical problem.

## Research Plan Shape

An NSF Phase I could include:

1. Formalize the technical architecture and measurable failure conditions.
2. Implement a research prototype and comparison baseline.
3. Test using synthetic and appropriately governed real-world data.
4. Quantify accuracy, information preservation, privacy, performance, and user-critical error rates.
5. Validate commercialization assumptions through customer and partner discovery.

## Review Criteria

NSF evaluates:

- Intellectual Merit
- Broader Impacts
- Commercial Potential

For ClearPath, Intellectual Merit requires a non-obvious technical contribution. Broader Impacts can include improved continuity, patient agency, access, safety, and reduced administrative burden. Commercial Potential requires a believable buyer, adoption route, competition analysis, regulatory plan, and scalable business model.

## Salary, Indirect Costs, And Fee

Reasonable founder and technical salaries may be budgeted for work actually performed. Compensation, indirect costs, subawards, consultants, travel, equipment, participant support, fee, and other categories must follow `NSF 26-510` and NSF proposal guidance.

NSF permits a small-business fee within its rules. Indirect costs should be calculated from a documented, consistent methodology. The company should establish timekeeping, cost allocation, accounting controls, and written compensation practices before award.

## Fast-Track Decision

Fast-Track combines a Phase I feasibility effort with a contingent Phase II development effort. It can reduce the gap between phases but requires a mature plan, stronger evidence, more PI effort, credible commercialization milestones, and a larger proposal burden.

ClearPath should begin with ordinary Phase I unless NSF staff recommends Fast-Track and the company has unusually strong preliminary evidence and partnerships.

## Contact And Pitch Questions

1. What technical uncertainty makes this more than routine health-software engineering?
2. What measurable result would prove feasibility in 12 months?
3. What existing exchange products or methods are the technical baselines?
4. Who pays, who adopts, and who can block adoption?
5. What data and pilot partners are available?
6. Is the founder prepared to meet the PI employment and effort requirements?

## Recommendation

Prepare an NSF Project Pitch in parallel with the NLM one-page aims. Submit only after an external technical reviewer can clearly state the innovation in one sentence and distinguish it from ordinary FHIR integration or records workflow software.
