# NIBIB Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

NIBIB supports broadly applicable biomedical engineering and technology, including health informatics and digital health. ClearPath can fit only if its proposal contributes a novel enabling technology, not merely a useful workflow application.

NLM is the safer institute choice. NIBIB becomes attractive if ClearPath's core research is a technically novel authorization, semantic normalization, provenance, trust, or privacy-preserving exchange method that can generalize across biomedical systems.

## Current Route

NIBIB participates in NIH small-business opportunities, including the current NIH Parent SBIR `PA-27-100`. The parent announcement is active through April 6, 2027, subject to participating-organization and institute rules.

The current Parent STTR `PA-27-102` may support a formal research-institution partnership if NIBIB is listed as participating for the chosen mechanism.

## NIBIB Interests Relevant To ClearPath

NIBIB identifies health informatics and digital health as areas of interest. Its informatics interests include technology for processing and evaluating complex biomedical information in real-world healthcare and patient-centered applications.

Possible ClearPath technical theses include:

- A machine-verifiable authorization model that binds patient intent to granular clinical data and destination
- Semantic and provenance-preserving transformation from heterogeneous source records into a canonical exchange package
- A trust architecture for cross-organization request, approval, delivery, revocation, and audit
- Privacy-preserving or safety-aware disclosure minimization across multiple data formats
- Novel verification methods for completeness, identity, provenance, and integrity of exchanged clinical information

## The Major Fit Test

NIBIB states that applying or testing already-developed tools, without a meaningful technology-development contribution, is outside its mission.

The following framing is likely too weak:

- Build an app where doctors request records
- Add patient approval screens
- Test whether users like it
- Connect common APIs

A stronger NIBIB framing would identify a hard technical limitation in current exchange systems, explain why existing methods cannot solve it, propose a new generalizable method, and test quantitative performance against appropriate baselines.

## Award Structure

- Phase I: NIBIB indicates a project period of no more than one year.
- Phase II: NIBIB indicates no more than two years.
- Phase IIB: NIBIB does not participate.
- Commercialization Readiness Pilot: NIBIB currently participates.

Budget, ownership, PI employment, workshare, registrations, and general application requirements follow the NIH parent announcement and SBA/NIH rules summarized in the NLM packet.

## Review And Milestones

NIBIB reviewers are likely to expect quantitative engineering milestones, such as:

- Authorization-policy accuracy and conflict handling
- Semantic mapping accuracy and information-loss measurement
- Provenance completeness
- Security and privacy threat-model results
- Transaction integrity under failure conditions
- Performance and scalability
- Human-factors error rates where humans interact with the technology
- Comparison with current standards-based approaches

The proposal must explain how the research advances biomedical technology beyond ClearPath's own product.

## Team Implications

The team should include credible expertise in:

- Biomedical informatics or computer science
- Health-data standards and terminology
- Security/privacy engineering
- Human factors
- Clinical workflow
- Statistics and study design

The founder may serve as SBIR PI if NIH employment and expertise rules are satisfied, but a technically ambitious NIBIB application needs experienced scientific leadership. A multiple-PI or senior key-person structure should be discussed with the program officer.

## Contact Questions

1. Is the proposed authorization/provenance method considered enabling biomedical technology or primarily health-services workflow?
2. What minimum level of technical novelty and preliminary evidence is expected for Phase I?
3. Would NLM be a more suitable primary assignment?
4. Which NIBIB program and study section would review the work?
5. Does the planned project fit NIBIB's one-year Phase I limit?
6. Would NIBIB accept a dual-assignment request with NLM?

## Recommendation

Schedule one NIBIB program conversation after the technical innovation statement is drafted. Submit to NIBIB only if staff confirms that the work is within mission and the proposal contains a substantial generalizable technology contribution.
