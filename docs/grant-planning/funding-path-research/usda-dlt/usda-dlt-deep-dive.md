# USDA Distance Learning And Telemedicine Deep Dive

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

USDA Rural Development's Distance Learning and Telemedicine Grant program supports deployment of telecommunications technology that improves rural healthcare or education. The FY2026 window is open through June 30, 2026.

This is not a research grant for building ClearPath from scratch. It can be useful when a rural healthcare consortium is prepared to deploy a sufficiently mature platform, equipment, network components, and related technical assistance.

## Current FY2026 Terms

- Application window: May 1 through June 30, 2026
- Estimated FY2026 program funding: approximately $27 million, plus potentially available prior-year funds
- Award range: $50,000 to $750,000
- Required nonfederal match: at least 15%
- Project period: three years from release of funds
- Submission: Grants.gov
- Geographic focus: rural areas with populations of 20,000 or fewer

The FY2026 NOFO, application guide, worksheets, and grant agreement control every cost and eligibility determination.

## Eligible Applicants

USDA lists eligible applicants that provide education or healthcare through telecommunications, including:

- State and local governments
- Federally recognized tribes
- Nonprofit organizations
- Incorporated for-profit businesses
- Consortia of eligible entities

ClearPath's legal entity might qualify in principle. That does not make a software-development proposal responsive. The applicant must demonstrate a rural telemedicine network and eligible purposes.

## Eligible Uses

The program may support:

- Audio, video, and interactive-video equipment
- Computer hardware
- Network components
- Software used for the distance-learning or telemedicine project
- Limited broadband facilities under program limits
- Instructional programming
- Limited technical assistance and user instruction

The program is primarily equipment- and deployment-oriented. Ordinary operating expenses, broad product R&D, unrelated salaries, and costs outside the approved rural project should not be assumed allowable.

## ClearPath Deployment Model

A potentially responsive future model would include:

- One or more rural hospitals, clinics, or community sites
- A hub or specialist organization
- Defined telemedicine encounters or care transitions
- Clear need for patient-authorized prior-record retrieval
- Eligible software and equipment at named sites
- A measurable access and service-delivery plan
- A nonfederal match commitment
- A mature enough ClearPath product to deploy safely

ClearPath could lead only after establishing the required network and operational capacity. A rural provider or consortium lead may be more credible, with ClearPath as vendor or project partner.

## Geographic Implications

The founder's Dallas address is not the service area. Eligible end-user sites must meet USDA's rural definitions. A Dallas-based applicant may still participate when the funded services reach qualifying rural communities.

Potential partner discovery should focus on communities outside the Dallas-Fort Worth urban core and verify every location using the FY2026 rules and USDA tools.

## Match Math

The minimum match is 15% and cannot come from another federal source. The exact method in the NOFO controls.

Illustrative planning only:

- $500,000 grant request may require at least $75,000 eligible nonfederal match if the percentage is applied to the grant amount under the current program formula.
- $750,000 grant request may require at least $112,500.

The grant guide must be used to calculate the actual match base. Do not rely on these illustrations in an application.
