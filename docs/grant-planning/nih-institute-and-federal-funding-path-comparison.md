# NIH Institute And Federal Funding Path Comparison

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Purpose

This document addresses the first two grant-planning questions for ClearPath:

1. Which NIH institute or center is the strongest home for the application?
2. How do NLM, NIBIB, AHRQ, and other relevant federal funding paths compare?

This is a program-fit analysis, not a final funding-opportunity selection. The exact active Notice of Funding Opportunity, due dates, budget limits, project period, and eligibility rules will be confirmed separately in the next research step.

## Current Product Used For This Analysis

ClearPath is a patient-controlled, interdisciplinary health information request and exchange platform.

The proposed Phase I work would research whether ClearPath can:

- Represent common record requests across clinical disciplines.
- Let patients understand and control section-level authorization.
- Generate consent-scoped, canonical JSON packages.
- Prevent denied or unauthorized information from entering a package.
- Preserve provenance, authorization, integrity, and audit history.
- Reduce confusion and administrative burden for providers and records staff.
- Establish feasibility with synthetic data before a real-PHI Phase II pilot.

ClearPath is not being positioned as a dental application, clinical decision support system, diagnostic system, or treatment recommendation product.

## Bottom-Line Recommendation

### Recommended Primary NIH Home: National Library Of Medicine

The National Library of Medicine is the strongest NIH institute for the current product without changing ClearPath's core mission.

NLM's current small-business interests explicitly support commercialization of biomedical informatics tools, methods, technologies, and software platforms. Its stated areas include biomedical data infrastructure, platform science, and human-centered biomedical informatics tools and methods.

Recommended NLM framing:

> ClearPath is a human-centered biomedical informatics platform for patient-controlled, consent-scoped exchange of health information across organizations and clinical disciplines.

Why NLM is the strongest choice:

- The product is fundamentally a biomedical informatics and information-exchange platform.
- The proposed research examines data representation, provenance, authorization fidelity, usability, and impact.
- The canonical JSON and future standards-translation architecture are central technical contributions.
- ClearPath can remain interdisciplinary rather than adopting a disease or specialty focus.
- NLM explicitly supports commercial software platforms and small-business awards.
- The application can present a credible research question without pretending ClearPath is a medical device.

Important NLM limitations:

- NLM does not accept requests to exceed the statutory budget guidelines through a budget-cap waiver.
- NLM currently does not accept Phase IIB SBIR or Commercialization Readiness Pilot projects.
- A competitive application must show a novel informatics method or platform contribution, not merely development of an attractive portal.

### Recommended Secondary NIH Conversation: NIBIB

NIBIB is a credible second choice through its Division of Health Informatics Technologies.

NIBIB supports science and technology for processing complex biomedical information to solve real-world healthcare problems. Its current small-business interests include health informatics, digital health, biomedical informatics, practical patient-centered applications, and broadly applicable technologies.

Recommended NIBIB framing:

> ClearPath is a broadly applicable health-informatics technology that introduces a consent-scoped canonical exchange architecture, deterministic authorization enforcement, and provenance-preserving translation methods for cross-system clinical information.

Why NIBIB could work:

- It explicitly supports informatics and digital-health technology.
- It values broadly applicable platforms rather than disease-specific products.
- ClearPath's canonical model, authorization engine, and validation methods could be framed as the core technical innovation.
- NIBIB generally supports Phase I project periods of one year or less, which matches the proposed Phase I schedule.

Why NIBIB is not the first recommendation:

- NIBIB states that merely applying and testing a previously developed tool is outside its mission.
- Reviewers may expect a stronger engineering or computational innovation than a workflow-centered platform currently demonstrates.
- ClearPath would need rigorous technical novelty in consent enforcement, provenance, schema translation, or interoperability validation.
- A software product whose main contribution is usability and care coordination may fit NLM or AHRQ more naturally.

### Strongest Scientific Mission Fit Outside NIH: AHRQ

The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality is not an NIH institute or center, but its Digital Healthcare Research program is exceptionally well aligned with ClearPath's purpose.

AHRQ's Digital Healthcare Research mission is to study how components of the digital healthcare ecosystem can improve healthcare delivery and create value for patients and families. Its current interests include digital healthcare safety, usability, implementation, patient empowerment, interoperability, and safe use by patients and professionals.

Recommended AHRQ framing:

> ClearPath is a patient-centered digital healthcare safety and care-coordination intervention designed to make cross-organization record requests more understandable, selective, auditable, and reliable.

Why AHRQ is scientifically strong:

- Patient safety and digital healthcare safety are central to the proposed authorization-fidelity research.
- The research measures usability, comprehension, implementation, workflow burden, and care coordination.
- AHRQ studies real-world healthcare delivery rather than only underlying technology.
- The product addresses patients, clinicians, records staff, and organizations together.

Important AHRQ limitation:

- AHRQ should not be assumed to provide the same NIH-style $300,000 Phase I SBIR path.
- The current AHRQ Digital Healthcare Research funding page highlights research announcements and special-emphasis notices, including digital healthcare safety, but the appropriate active mechanism must be confirmed.
- Some AHRQ mechanisms may be better suited to a research institution or a small business working with an experienced health-services research partner.
- The current AI safety R18 announcement specifically does not support development of a new AI system and is not the right mechanism for ClearPath as currently defined.

## Ranked NIH Institutes And Centers

### Tier 1: Best Current Fit

#### 1. National Library Of Medicine

Overall fit: Excellent

Small-business fit: Excellent

Product distortion required: Low

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Biomedical informatics platform
- Human-centered information tools
- Canonical health-data model
- Consent-scoped exchange
- Data provenance and integrity
- Interoperability methods
- Commercial software platform

Main risk:

- The application is rejected as routine software development unless the research contribution and technical uncertainty are made explicit.

Recommendation:

- Contact NLM first.
- Prepare a one-page Specific Aims summary centered on informatics feasibility.

#### 2. National Institute Of Biomedical Imaging And Bioengineering

Overall fit: Strong

Small-business fit: Strong

Product distortion required: Moderate

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Broadly applicable biomedical informatics technology
- Novel information-processing architecture
- Consent enforcement and package validation
- Cross-format translation
- Practical patient-centered digital health

Main risk:

- Insufficient engineering novelty for NIBIB's expectations.

Recommendation:

- Contact the NIBIB Small Business Team after NLM.
- Ask whether the proposed authorization and provenance architecture is sufficiently within NIBIB's informatics portfolio.

### Tier 2: Strong Conditional Fits

#### 3. National Institute On Minority Health And Health Disparities

Overall fit: Strong only with a genuine disparities research aim

Small-business fit: Strong

Product distortion required: Moderate to high

NIMHD's current small-business interests explicitly include:

- Healthcare access and quality
- Care coordination and patient engagement
- Patient-provider communication
- Digital health
- Electronic health records and biomedical informatics
- Rehabilitation and assistive technologies
- Technologies supporting community-level health services

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Fragmented record exchange as a barrier for underserved populations
- Patient-controlled communication across safety-net and specialty care
- Accessibility, affordability, health literacy, and digital inclusion
- Care coordination involving federally qualified health centers or other safety-net providers

Main risk:

- ClearPath does not currently have a defined health-disparities population, hypothesis, or outcome.
- Adding disparity language only to improve funding fit would be scientifically weak and inappropriate.

Recommendation:

- Consider NIMHD only if ClearPath commits to a real access or disparities research program and recruits appropriate community or safety-net partners.

#### 4. National Center For Advancing Translational Sciences

Overall fit: Moderate to strong for research-data exchange; moderate for routine clinical exchange

Small-business fit: Strong

Product distortion required: Moderate

NCATS currently supports biomedical, clinical, and health-research informatics, tools for sharing research data, platforms integrating patient data, and technologies improving human-subjects research.

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Consent-scoped information exchange for multi-site clinical and translational research
- Patient-reported data integration
- Research participant authorization and data provenance
- Tools lowering barriers to meaningful sharing of research data

Main risk:

- ClearPath's current customer and workflow are healthcare organizations exchanging records for care, not researchers conducting translational science.
- Reframing the product around clinical research could pull the company away from its intended market.

Recommendation:

- Keep NCATS as a backup unless the product develops a genuine clinical-research exchange use case.

#### 5. National Institute On Aging

Overall fit: Strong if the first market focuses on older adults and caregivers

Small-business fit: Strong

Product distortion required: Moderate

NIA currently supports digital health, care delivery, care coordination, aging in place, and technologies that reduce caregiver burden. It also provides relatively generous Phase I and Phase II budget guidance for projects within its mission.

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Care transitions for older adults
- Caregiver-mediated authorization
- Hospital-to-rehabilitation record exchange
- Medication and discharge-information continuity
- Reducing caregiver burden

Main risk:

- The product would need an older-adult or aging research focus rather than remaining entirely population-neutral.

Recommendation:

- Consider NIA if founder interviews show that older-adult care transitions and caregiver coordination are the strongest initial market.

#### 6. National Institute Of Nursing Research

Overall fit: Moderate

Small-business fit: Moderate

Product distortion required: Moderate to high

NINR currently emphasizes conditions of daily life, multilevel approaches, chronic conditions, community engagement, health disparities, and technological advances that improve healthcare outcomes.

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Nurse-led care coordination
- Chronic-disease transitions
- Community and patient engagement
- Digital health addressing disparities

Main risk:

- ClearPath is not currently a nursing intervention, chronic-disease intervention, or conditions-of-daily-life intervention.
- NINR does not currently accept Phase IIB or Commercialization Readiness Pilot projects.

Recommendation:

- Keep NINR as a conditional path if nursing-led coordination becomes central to the pilot design.

## NIH Institutes And Offices Not Recommended For The Current Application

### NIDCR

Not recommended because ClearPath is no longer a dental or craniofacial product.

### Disease Institutes Without A Defined Disease Use Case

NEI, NIAID, NIAMS, NIDCD, NIEHS, NINDS, NIAAA, and similar disease or organ-system institutes should not be targeted unless ClearPath intentionally chooses a research problem within that institute's mission.

### NIGMS

Not recommended as the primary home. NIGMS is not the natural institute for a commercial patient-controlled record-exchange product. General informatics language alone does not make it a good fit.

### NIH Office Of The Director Programs

Programs such as All of Us, the Office of Data Science Strategy, and NIH Common Fund may influence standards or create future collaboration opportunities, but they are not the most direct primary assignment for the proposed small-business application.

## Other Federal Funding Paths

### 1. AHRQ Digital Healthcare Research

Scientific fit: Excellent

Near-term funding-path certainty: Moderate to low until an active mechanism is confirmed

Best for:

- Digital healthcare safety
- Usability and implementation
- Patient engagement
- Care coordination
- Workflow and health-services research

Recommendation:

- Contact AHRQ Digital Healthcare Research staff in parallel with NLM.
- Do not build the $300,000 Phase I budget around AHRQ until the mechanism is verified.

### 2. NSF America's Seed Fund

Scientific fit: Moderate

Commercial technology fit: Strong if ClearPath demonstrates deep technical innovation

NSF supports early-stage R&D across technology areas, takes no equity, and evaluates innovativeness, commercial potential, and societal impact. It is less tied to a particular disease mission than NIH.

Best ClearPath emphasis:

- Novel privacy-preserving authorization architecture
- Standards-neutral data transformation
- Verifiable consent enforcement
- Secure distributed exchange
- Generalizable technical innovation with markets beyond one clinical workflow

Main risk:

- Ordinary application development is not deep technology.
- ClearPath needs a defensible technical invention, not only a healthcare business idea.

Recommendation:

- Keep NSF as a serious alternative if the technical lead can define the core research innovation crisply.

### 3. ARPA-H

Scientific fit: Conceptually relevant to integrated health systems

Stage fit: Weak for the current company stage

ARPA-H funds high-impact projects intended to solve major health challenges that traditional research or industry cannot. Its current Resilient Systems and Scalable Solutions mission-office opportunities concern integrated healthcare systems, data, information, and equitable scale.

Main risk:

- ARPA-H expects unusually ambitious, high-impact, milestone-driven programs.
- Awards and project scope are generally much larger than ClearPath's current Phase I plan.
- Current opportunities are topic-specific and not a standing substitute for the NIH SBIR parent mechanism.

Recommendation:

- Monitor ARPA-H, but do not make it the primary near-term application.

### 4. ASTP/ONC Health IT Funding

Scientific and policy fit: Excellent

Funding reliability: Low because opportunities are intermittent

The Assistant Secretary for Technology Policy/Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology leads federal work involving interoperability, USCDI, FHIR, TEFCA, patient access, health IT usability, information blocking, and secure exchange.

Recommendation:

- Monitor funding announcements, cooperative agreements, challenges, and contracts.
- Use ASTP/ONC standards and policy as design requirements even when it is not the funder.
- Do not assume a standing annual Phase I startup grant exists.

### 5. HRSA

Scientific fit: Conditional

Applicant fit: Usually stronger through an eligible healthcare or community partner

Potential ClearPath uses:

- Rural health
- Health-center care coordination
- Maternal and child health
- Workforce or telehealth access

Dallas implication:

- Dallas itself is not a rural pilot setting.
- A rural or underserved program could still be possible through an eligible partner serving the relevant population, but location claims must be based on the specific program's eligibility rules.

Recommendation:

- Consider only with an eligible provider or community organization and a program-specific use case.

### 6. CDC SBIR

Scientific fit: Low for the current product

Potential fit:

- Public-health reporting
- Immunization exchange
- Chronic-disease prevention
- Emergency or outbreak information exchange

Current program limitations listed by HHS include no STTR and no Fast-Track applications for CDC.

Recommendation:

- Do not prioritize unless the product develops a defined public-health exchange use case.

### 7. FDA SBIR

Scientific fit: Low

FDA small-business interests center on regulatory science, medical products, safety, diagnostics, and regulated devices. ClearPath is not currently being developed as a medical device or diagnostic product.

Recommendation:

- Do not prioritize.

### 8. CMS Innovation Center

Scientific fit: Moderate for future care-delivery demonstrations

Startup grant fit: Low

CMS Innovation Center tests payment and service-delivery models. It is not a routine early-stage software R&D grant program.

Recommendation:

- Treat CMS payment models and quality measures as future commercialization context, not the current Phase I funding source.

### 9. Department Of Veterans Affairs Or Department Of Defense

Scientific fit: Conditional

Potential fit:

- Veteran or military care transitions
- Community-care record exchange
- Complex cross-system coordination

Recommendation:

- Monitor targeted SBIR solicitations and contracts.
- Pursue only when a solicitation directly matches ClearPath and the company has an appropriate veteran or military-health partner.

## Dallas Location Analysis

### What Dallas Does Not Change

- NIH institute assignment is based primarily on scientific mission and application content, not the founder's state.
- ClearPath can apply to NLM, NIBIB, or another participating institute from Dallas.
- The company should use its current Dallas address consistently in registrations and the application.
- Moving from Utah to Texas does not require choosing a Texas-specific NIH institute.

### What Dallas Can Improve

Dallas provides a large, diverse healthcare and research environment that may support:

- Clinical workflow interviews
- Patient and caregiver usability research
- Health-system or practice letters of support
- Security, informatics, and health-services research advisors
- Potential STTR research-institution collaboration
- A discipline-diverse Phase II pilot network

Potential organizations to investigate, without implying any current relationship:

- UT Southwestern Medical Center
- Parkland Health
- University of Texas at Dallas
- Southern Methodist University
- Baylor Scott & White Health
- Children's Health
- Texas Health Resources
- Methodist Health System
- Independent primary care and specialty practices
- Community and safety-net health organizations

Each organization has its own research, contracting, compliance, and partnership requirements. No organization should be named in an application as a partner until it agrees and provides appropriate documentation.

### Dallas-Specific Strategic Options

Option A: NLM with discipline-diverse Dallas interview and usability partners.

This is the recommended default.

Option B: NLM STTR with a Dallas-area nonprofit research institution.

This may strengthen methods and human-subjects infrastructure, but it requires a qualifying research partner and compliance with STTR work-allocation rules.

Option C: NIMHD with a genuine Dallas safety-net or underserved-population research partnership.

This should be pursued only if health disparities become a real central aim rather than grant language added after the fact.

Option D: NIA with an older-adult care-transition and caregiver focus.

This is viable if discovery research identifies older-adult transitions as the strongest initial use case.

## Questions For NLM

1. Does a patient-controlled, consent-scoped interoffice health-information exchange platform fit NLM's biomedical informatics small-business interests?
2. Is the proposed research on authorization fidelity, provenance, canonical data representation, and human-centered usability sufficiently research-oriented for Phase I?
3. Should the application emphasize biomedical data infrastructure, human-centered informatics, or both?
4. Would NLM prefer an SBIR application led by the company or an STTR application with a health-services or informatics research institution?
5. Does the proposed synthetic-data Phase I with participant usability research create an appropriate feasibility package for Phase II?
6. Are there NLM-funded tools, standards, datasets, or communities the project should build upon?

## Questions For NIBIB

1. Is a consent-scoped canonical exchange and validation architecture within the Division of Health Informatics Technologies portfolio?
2. What level of technical novelty would distinguish this project from implementation of an existing health IT workflow?
3. Would NIBIB view authorization enforcement, provenance preservation, and standards translation as sufficiently broadly applicable bioengineering or informatics innovations?
4. Would the human-factors research belong within the technical Phase I approach?

## Questions For AHRQ

1. Which current funding mechanism best supports development and feasibility testing of a patient-controlled interdisciplinary record-request workflow?
2. Can a small business apply directly, or would a research-institution partnership be more appropriate?
3. Does the project fit AHRQ's digital healthcare safety special emphasis when the safety question is unintended disclosure and misunderstanding of authorization?
4. Which outcomes would AHRQ prioritize: safety, comprehension, staff burden, request completion time, care coordination, or implementation feasibility?

## Decision Rule

ClearPath should select the institute that supports the product it actually intends to build.

The current decision rule is:

- Choose NLM unless its program staff advise that the project is outside scope.
- Choose NIBIB only if the technical innovation is strong enough for its informatics mission.
- Choose NIMHD, NIA, NINR, NIMH, NIDA, NICHD, or a disease institute only when that population or clinical context is a genuine central research aim.
- Pursue AHRQ in parallel when a suitable active mechanism is identified.
- Keep NSF as the strongest non-HHS startup alternative if ClearPath can articulate a deep technical invention.
