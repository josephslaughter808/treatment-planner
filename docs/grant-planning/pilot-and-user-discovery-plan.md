# Pilot And User Discovery Plan

## Purpose

ClearPath needs evidence that providers, office staff, and patients understand and value the record request and consent workflow.

Phase I should validate the workflow with synthetic or de-identified examples. Phase II should support controlled pilots with real-world organizations once legal, security, and compliance requirements are satisfied.

## Discovery Goals

Phase I discovery should answer:

1. Do providers recognize the problem ClearPath solves?
2. Which record requests happen most often across offices and disciplines?
3. Do patients understand what they are approving?
4. Can office staff use the workflow without heavy training?
5. Does the JSON package and human-readable summary support provider review?
6. What would make a practice willing to pilot or buy ClearPath?

## Target Participants

Recommended Phase I participant groups:

- Dentists and oral surgeons
- Primary care providers
- Specialists who request outside records
- Office managers
- Front desk or records staff
- Patients or caregivers
- Health IT or interoperability advisors

The first discovery group can stay small. Quality of feedback matters more than participant count in Phase I.

## Priority Use Cases

Phase I should test a few high-value scenarios:

### Scenario 1: Dental Or Oral Surgery Medical Clearance

Requesting provider needs medication history, allergies, conditions, surgical history, and clearance context before treatment.

Why it matters:

This scenario is concrete, familiar, and connects the original dental workflow to the broader medical communication platform.

### Scenario 2: Specialist Requests Prior Records

A specialist needs prior diagnoses, labs, imaging reports, medication list, and treatment notes from another provider.

Why it matters:

This demonstrates interdisciplinary exchange beyond dentistry.

### Scenario 3: Patient-Mediated Records Release

A patient receives a request, limits sections, approves the release, and later views or revokes access.

Why it matters:

This is the core patient-controlled grant story.

## Discovery Methods

### Interviews

Use 30-45 minute conversations to learn:

- Current record request process
- Pain points
- Delays and failure modes
- Consent concerns
- Staff burden
- Buying and adoption barriers

### Prototype Walkthroughs

Show participants:

- Provider request creation
- Patient approval screen
- Consent-scoped package output
- Provider review screen
- Audit trail

Ask participants to narrate what they think is happening and where they feel confused.

### Task-Based Usability Tests

Give users simple tasks:

- Create a record request.
- Approve only selected sections.
- Deny a request.
- Review an approved package.
- Find request status.

Measure:

- Completion
- Confusion points
- Time to complete
- Trust concerns
- Missing information

## Phase I Research Outputs

Deliverables:

- Interview guide
- Prototype walkthrough script
- Participant feedback summary
- Workflow friction list
- Consent language issues
- Provider value proposition notes
- Phase II pilot recommendations

## Phase I Metrics

Possible validation metrics:

- Number of stakeholder interviews completed
- Percent of providers who recognize the problem as important
- Percent of patients who understand the request and approval screen
- Percent of office staff who can explain request status
- Most requested record categories
- Top workflow barriers
- Top security/privacy concerns

## Phase II Pilot Plan

Phase II should include controlled pilots after security, legal, and compliance requirements are met.

Pilot goals:

- Use ClearPath in real office workflows.
- Measure request completion time.
- Measure patient approval/denial rates.
- Measure staff time saved.
- Measure provider satisfaction with received package.
- Identify compliance and support needs.

Potential pilot structure:

1. Synthetic-data rehearsal with pilot staff.
2. Limited live workflow with approved agreements.
3. Small number of controlled patients or record requests.
4. Weekly monitoring and feedback.
5. Final pilot outcomes report.

## Participant Safety And Privacy

Phase I should avoid collecting PHI unless the project has required approvals.

Guidelines:

- Use synthetic examples in interviews.
- Do not paste patient information into notes.
- Avoid screenshots with real patient data.
- De-identify quotes and findings.
- Store research notes securely.
- Confirm grant manager guidance on human subjects or IRB requirements.

## Discovery Success Criteria

Phase I discovery is successful if:

- Providers confirm record request friction is real and important.
- Patients can understand and control approval.
- Office staff can understand request status and package review.
- The prototype reveals a believable path to Phase II pilot.
- Feedback supports the grant's commercialization and public health rationale.
