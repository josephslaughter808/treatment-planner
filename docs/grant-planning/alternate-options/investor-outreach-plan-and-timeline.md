# Investor Outreach Plan And Timeline

## Objective

Build investor relationships without prematurely advertising securities or accepting unfavorable money. The process should produce either a well-structured pre-seed round or enough market feedback to delay the raise intelligently.

## Phase 1: Readiness Sprint

### Weeks 1-4

- Confirm entity, cap table, IP, and counsel needs.
- Recruit a technical advisor or technical-lead candidate.
- Complete ten high-priority customer interviews.
- Refine the first customer segment and value proposition.
- Build a concise product demo using synthetic data.
- Draft an 18-month budget and milestone plan.
- Prepare a ten- to twelve-slide deck.
- Create a one-page company summary.

### Weeks 5-8

- Reach at least 25-30 total customer and stakeholder interviews.
- Seek two detailed pilot-interest letters.
- Obtain independent technical and security architecture feedback.
- Complete the data-room foundation.
- Build a list of 75-125 relevant investors and accelerators.
- Request warm introductions without publicly offering securities.
- Submit or advance priority grant concepts.

## Phase 2: Investor Discovery

### Weeks 9-12

Meet 10-15 friendly investors, operators, and accelerator leaders for feedback before declaring a formal financing process.

Ask:

- What evidence is missing?
- Is the buyer credible?
- Is the regulatory plan believable?
- Which milestone would change investment interest?
- Is the proposed raise large enough to reach that milestone?
- What terms and ownership would this stage support?

Track answers. Do not change the company after every meeting; look for repeated patterns.

## Phase 3: Formal Raise

### Months 4-6

After counsel selects the securities exemption:

- Contact investors in coordinated weekly groups.
- Prioritize warm introductions and healthcare expertise.
- Schedule meetings close together to create a real decision window.
- Send the deck only after confirming relevance.
- Open data-room access after serious interest.
- Track check size, stage, thesis, decision process, objections, next step, and references.
- Seek a credible lead or anchor investor before filling small checks.
- Compare the whole term package, not only valuation cap.

Fundraising can take longer than three months. The company must maintain enough runway to avoid negotiating in crisis.

## Investor Pipeline

### Tier 1: Strategic Angels

- Healthcare executives
- Physicians with health-system leadership experience
- Health-information-exchange and interoperability operators
- Healthcare SaaS founders
- Security and compliance leaders

Desired contribution: domain credibility, customer introductions, hiring help, and realistic procurement advice.

### Tier 2: Texas Networks And Accelerators

- Health Wildcatters
- North Texas Angel Network
- Capital Factory
- Texas Medical Center Innovation programs
- Provider-connected healthtech accelerators

Verify application status, investment terms, equity, fees, location requirements, and portfolio conflicts directly.

### Tier 3: Specialist Funds

Target pre-seed and seed funds whose published portfolios show:

- Digital health infrastructure
- Healthcare workflow software
- Interoperability
- Patient access
- Enterprise healthcare SaaS
- Regulated-data security

Avoid sending generic outreach to every fund with “health” on its website.

### Tier 4: Strategic Companies

Potential strategic categories include EHR vendors, release-of-information companies, health-information networks, payers, provider groups, and healthcare-service organizations.

Approach after counsel and competitive analysis. Do not grant broad exclusivity, rights of first refusal, unrestricted data access, or control over future partnerships without understanding the long-term cost.

## Pitch Deck Outline

1. ClearPath and one-sentence mission
2. Fragmented record-request problem
3. Current workflow and harm
4. Product demonstration and patient-controlled authorization
5. Why existing portals, HIEs, and release vendors do not fully solve it
6. Initial customer and use case
7. Technical and standards architecture
8. Research, pilot, and grant plan
9. Market and business model
10. Team and missing hires
11. Milestones and use of funds
12. Financing request and contact

Do not put unsupported market size, customer logos, compliance claims, or performance numbers in the deck.

## Meeting Narrative

The founder should be able to explain:

- Why this problem matters personally and economically
- What has been built
- What remains unproven
- Why patient-mediated authorization is differentiated
- Who buys first
- Why existing exchange infrastructure is insufficient
- What $750,000 accomplishes
- Why the company can reach a grant, pilot, revenue, or next-round milestone before cash runs out

Investors generally respect precise uncertainty more than inflated certainty.

## Weekly Metrics

- New qualified investor introductions
- First meetings
- Second meetings
- Data-room requests
- Diligence calls
- Soft-circled dollars
- Signed documents and received funds
- Common objections
- Customer and pilot progress
- Remaining runway

Soft commitments are not cash. Do not hire against them.

## Decision Rules

Pause or revise the round if:

- Twenty qualified meetings produce no second meetings.
- Investors consistently reject the buyer or use case.
- The technical-lead gap blocks diligence.
- Counsel or corporate cleanup reveals material issues.
- A grant decision provides enough runway to reach a substantially better valuation.

Proceed when:

- A credible investor anchors the round.
- Terms preserve grant eligibility and founder control.
- The amount reaches defined milestones with contingency.
- The investor references are strong.
- The company understands every right and dilution consequence.
