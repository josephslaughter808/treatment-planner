# Investor Readiness And Due Diligence

## Goal

An investor should be able to understand the company, verify ownership and risk, and make a decision without discovering preventable legal or financial problems.

## Corporate Foundation

- [ ] Confirm the current legal entity, jurisdiction, tax status, and good standing.
- [ ] Decide with counsel whether a Delaware C corporation is appropriate before institutional fundraising.
- [ ] Document founder stock or membership ownership correctly.
- [ ] Create an accurate fully diluted cap table.
- [ ] Document all verbal or written equity promises.
- [ ] Adopt bylaws or operating agreement, board consents, and required governance records.
- [ ] Open company bank and accounting accounts separate from personal funds.
- [ ] Obtain EIN and required state registrations.
- [ ] Review founder vesting and any required tax elections with counsel immediately; some elections have short deadlines.

Do not convert or reincorporate solely because a template says investors prefer Delaware. Entity conversion has legal, tax, IP, and grant-eligibility consequences.

## Intellectual Property

- [ ] Founder assigns relevant code, designs, documentation, domains, and inventions to the company.
- [ ] Every developer, contractor, advisor, and employee signs confidentiality and invention-assignment terms.
- [ ] Identify code or content created before company formation.
- [ ] Inventory open-source dependencies and licenses.
- [ ] Remove unlicensed assets, copied code, test data, and credentials.
- [ ] Document trademarks, domains, repositories, cloud accounts, and administrator access.
- [ ] Establish a patent/trade-secret strategy with qualified counsel if technically justified.
- [ ] Confirm grant or partner terms will not conflict with investor rights or background IP.

## Product And Technical Diligence

- [ ] Repeatable local and production builds
- [ ] Automated tests and current security dependency review
- [ ] Architecture diagram and data-flow map
- [ ] Clear boundary between prototype and production-ready components
- [ ] Synthetic/de-identified demo data only until approvals support PHI
- [ ] Threat model and prioritized security backlog
- [ ] Backup, recovery, logging, monitoring, and incident-response plans
- [ ] Interoperability roadmap grounded in actual vendor access
- [ ] Technical-debt and hiring assessment from an experienced independent engineer
- [ ] No secrets or patient data committed to source control

## Healthcare, Privacy, And Regulatory Diligence

- [ ] Written analysis of ClearPath's role under HIPAA for each business model
- [ ] BAA and data-use agreement strategy
- [ ] State privacy and medical-record authorization review
- [ ] Minor, guardian, dependent, caregiver, and sensitive-record analysis
- [ ] Information-blocking and interoperability analysis
- [ ] FDA device-status analysis if product claims change
- [ ] Human-subjects and IRB plan for research
- [ ] Security risk analysis and roadmap before real PHI
- [ ] Accessibility plan
- [ ] Cyber, technology E&O, general liability, and D&O insurance plan

Investors will view “we are HIPAA compliant” without evidence as a risk signal. Describe current status and planned controls accurately.

## Market And Commercial Diligence

- [ ] Define the first customer segment.
- [ ] Identify economic buyer, daily users, patient role, procurement owner, and blocker.
- [ ] Complete at least 30 structured discovery interviews.
- [ ] Quantify current records-request time, cost, delay, and failure rates.
- [ ] Map direct competitors, HIEs, release-of-information vendors, EHR tools, TEFCA/QHIN services, and substitutes.
- [ ] Test pricing and contracting assumptions.
- [ ] Obtain pilot-interest letters with specific workflow and next steps.
- [ ] Build a bottom-up market model rather than relying only on a large national TAM.
- [ ] Document healthcare sales-cycle assumptions.

## Financial Diligence

- [ ] Historical company spending and founder contributions
- [ ] Clean bookkeeping
- [ ] Current balance sheet and cash position
- [ ] 18-month monthly budget and hiring plan
- [ ] Grant and investor funds separated by cost objective
- [ ] Three scenarios: lean, base, and delayed-pilot
- [ ] Payroll, contractor, and tax compliance plan
- [ ] Federal award accounting readiness
- [ ] No undisclosed debt, liens, obligations, or related-party transactions

## Fundraising And Securities

- [ ] Retain startup securities counsel before offering or selling securities.
- [ ] Select the offering exemption before outreach becomes an offer.
- [ ] Determine whether communications constitute general solicitation.
- [ ] Use a counsel-reviewed SAFE, note, or priced-round package.
- [ ] Complete investor accreditation process required by the exemption.
- [ ] Obtain board and stockholder approvals as required.
- [ ] File Form D and state notices on time when applicable.
- [ ] Track every investor communication, document, signature, and payment.
- [ ] Model SAFE, option-pool, and later-round dilution.
- [ ] Recheck NIH/NSF small-business ownership rules before closing.

## Data Room Structure

1. Corporate formation and governance
2. Capitalization and securities
3. Intellectual property
4. Product and technical architecture
5. Security, privacy, and compliance
6. Market, customer discovery, and pilots
7. Financial statements and forecast
8. Team, employment, and contractors
9. Grant applications and government registrations
10. Material contracts, insurance, disputes, and risks

Give access in stages. Do not place credentials, unnecessary PHI, privileged legal advice, or unrestricted sensitive security details in a general investor data room.

## Readiness Gate

Do not formally launch the round until:

- The entity and cap table are clean.
- The company owns its IP.
- The founder can explain the problem, buyer, evidence, and moat concisely.
- A technical leader or credible recruitment plan exists.
- At least two concrete pilot prospects exist.
- The 18-month use of funds is costed.
- Counsel has approved the offering process and documents.
