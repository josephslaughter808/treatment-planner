# Investor Funding Strategy

## Review Date

June 20, 2026

## Executive Summary

Investor funding is a realistic alternate path, but ClearPath is not yet positioned for a strong institutional venture round. It is positioned to begin relationship-building and could become ready for a healthcare-angel or pre-seed SAFE round after a focused preparation sprint.

The best strategy is probably not grant or investor. It is a hybrid in which:

- Grant funding supports defined feasibility research, scientific personnel, and approved research expenses.
- Equity funding supports commercial product work, company formation, recruiting, fundraising, sales discovery, production security, and other activities that may be outside or larger than the grant scope.
- The company prevents duplicate charging and discloses overlapping support as required.

## Current Company Stage

Based on the repository and planning documents, ClearPath currently has:

- A functioning software prototype
- A defined interdisciplinary patient-controlled record-request concept
- Early canonical package, authorization, and audit architecture
- A substantial research plan
- A Phase I budget and staffing plan
- Grant and agency research

ClearPath does not yet appear to have documented:

- Paying customers or recurring revenue
- Completed clinical pilots
- Signed pilot agreements
- A production environment approved for live PHI
- Independent penetration testing
- A full-time experienced technical leader
- A completed healthcare legal and regulatory analysis
- Demonstrated reduction in records delay, cost, or patient harm
- A clean investor-ready corporate and IP package

This is a pre-seed profile. Investors will primarily underwrite founder quality, problem insight, technical differentiation, market potential, and evidence that the next milestones are achievable.

## Recommended Raise

### Balanced Option

- Target: $750,000
- Instrument: post-money SAFE with valuation cap and no discount
- Planning cap: approximately $7 million
- Expected SAFE ownership at that cap: approximately 10.7% before later priced-round dilution
- Intended runway: approximately 15 to 18 months, depending on hiring and vendor costs

### Lean Option

- Target: $500,000
- Intended use: technical lead, founder runway, legal/security foundation, customer discovery, and grant bridge
- Risk: may be too little for production security and a credible healthcare pilot

### Full Pre-Seed Option

- Target: $1 million
- Intended use: stronger team, production hardening, partner integrations, security work, and controlled pilot readiness
- Risk: harder to raise at the current stage and creates materially more dilution

The raise amount should be derived from milestones and monthly burn, not from the maximum investors might offer.

## Milestones The Round Must Buy

The round should end with evidence, not merely more features:

1. Investor-ready corporation, cap table, IP ownership, accounting, and governance.
2. Full-time technical leadership.
3. At least 30 structured customer and workflow interviews.
4. At least two credible pilot-interest letters or agreements.
5. Validated authorization, provenance, and exchange prototype using synthetic or de-identified data.
6. Independent architecture, privacy, and threat-model review.
7. Production security roadmap and costed path to a real-PHI pilot.
8. Clear first customer segment and pricing hypothesis.
9. Grant submission or award progress.
10. A next-round or revenue plan supported by measurable traction.

## Instrument Choice

### Recommended: Post-Money SAFE

The post-money SAFE makes the ownership sold through the SAFE round easier to estimate. A valuation-cap, no-discount form is common and simpler than stacking different discounts, caps, and MFN terms.

Advantages:

- Lower legal and negotiation burden than a priced round
- Flexible rolling closes
- No maturity date or interest like a convertible note
- Ownership sold through the SAFE is easier to model

Risks:

- It is still a security and requires legal compliance
- Multiple SAFEs can create substantial hidden dilution
- Conversion mechanics can surprise founders
- Later option-pool and priced-round dilution still applies
- Side letters and pro-rata rights can complicate the cap table

Use current counsel-reviewed documents. Do not download a template, change terms casually, and begin taking checks.

### Convertible Note

A note creates debt, interest, and a maturity date. It may be useful when investors require it, but it adds repayment and maturity pressure that ClearPath does not need.

### Priced Preferred Equity

A priced round establishes a share price and typically includes a stock-purchase agreement, amended charter, investor rights, governance terms, and more legal expense. It becomes more appropriate when a lead investor is prepared to fund a larger seed round and the company has traction supporting formal negotiation.

### Regulation Crowdfunding

Regulation Crowdfunding can reach non-accredited investors and permit up to $5 million in a rolling 12-month period through a registered intermediary. It requires public Form C disclosures, platform fees, financial-statement work, campaign execution, ongoing reports, and cap-table planning.

It is not the first recommendation. ClearPath handles a sensitive healthcare trust problem, and a poorly prepared public campaign could create reputational and disclosure burdens before the company has validation.

## Securities Route

For a private angel SAFE round, counsel may recommend Rule 506(b) of Regulation D:

- Unlimited capital may be raised primarily from accredited investors.
- General solicitation and public advertising are prohibited.
- Form D is generally due to the SEC within 15 days after the first sale.
- State notice filings and fees may still apply.
- Texas requires a Rule 506 notice and fee through its filing process within the applicable period after the first Texas sale.

Rule 506(c) permits broad solicitation, but every purchaser must be accredited and the company must take reasonable verification steps. The company should not publicly advertise an investment round until counsel selects and documents the offering exemption.

## Investor Types

Priority order:

1. Healthcare operators and physician executives who understand interoperability and buying cycles
2. Healthtech angel groups and syndicates
3. Dallas and Texas early-stage investors
4. Healthcare accelerators with clinical and investor access
5. Pre-seed healthtech funds
6. Strategic healthcare companies, approached cautiously

Strategic investors may request exclusivity, commercial rights, data access, board influence, or restrictions that reduce future options. Strategic money is not automatically better money.

## Texas Starting Points

Current ecosystem candidates for research and relationship-building include:

- Health Wildcatters in Dallas
- North Texas Angel Network
- Capital Factory
- Texas Medical Center Innovation ecosystem
- Healthcare Technology Accelerator programs with provider partners

Participation terms, current applications, investment amounts, and fit must be checked directly. An accelerator should be evaluated by equity cost, cash investment, mentor quality, clinical access, follow-on history, required relocation, and contractual restrictions.

## Grant And Investor Interaction

Equity financing does not normally disqualify an otherwise eligible small business from SBIR. However:

- Ownership after financing must continue to satisfy the selected SBIR/STTR rules.
- Venture-capital ownership can affect eligibility under some mechanisms.
- The PI employment requirement still applies.
- Federal funds and investor funds cannot pay the same cost.
- Other support, overlap, and foreign relationships must be disclosed as required.
- Investor rights cannot prevent the company from fulfilling award terms, data rights, domestic work, or commercialization obligations.

Counsel and the grant program should review the cap table before accepting an investment that could affect eligibility.

## Recommendation

Begin investor preparation and conversations now, but delay a formal raise until the company has completed the readiness checklist, identified its corporate structure, assigned all IP, recruited credible technical leadership, and obtained at least early pilot interest. Seek grant and investor capital in parallel without letting either process distort the research question or product mission.
